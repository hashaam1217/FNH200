# Lesson 03: Fat and Sugar Substitutes; Sensory Perception — Study Deck

35 flashcards · 12 cloze · 15 MCQs

## Flashcards

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How many Calories per gram does fat provide, and how does that compare to other food components? | Fat provides 9 Cal per gram, making it the most calorie-dense component in food. |
| 2 | Fat substitutes are grouped into which three types, and what is each type classified by? | They are classified by what they are made of: protein-based, carbohydrate-based, and fat-based. |
| 3 | Simplesse: what type of fat substitute is it, what is it made from, and how digestible is it? | A protein-based fat substitute made from soy, milk or egg white protein. It is partially digestible. |
| 4 | What is microparticulation? | The process used to make Simplesse, in which protein is formed into tiny spherical particles. These protein particles stabilize and give a fat-like creamy texture to food. |
| 5 | How may Simplesse appear in a product's ingredient list? | Products made with Simplesse do NOT have to be labelled "Simplesse"; it may be listed as "modified protein." |
| 6 | Can the fat used to make deep-fried products, such as French fries, be replaced by Simplesse? | No. Simplesse is protein-based and cannot withstand deep-frying; Olean is the fat substitute that can replace deep-frying fat. |
| 7 | Maltrins: what type of fat substitute are they, what are they chemically, and how digestible are they? | Carbohydrate-based fat substitutes, also called maltodextrins (short chains of starch). They are fully digestible. |
| 8 | Olean (generic name olestra): what type of fat substitute is it and what is it chemically? | A fat-based fat substitute; chemically it is a combination of "sugar" and "fat." |
| 9 | Olean/olestra: digestibility, calories, and heat behaviour? | Indigestible, so it contributes no calories. It is heat stable and can be used for deep-frying (e.g., fat-free Pringles). |
| 10 | Match Simplesse, Maltrin and Olean with indigestible / partially digestible / fully digestible. | Simplesse = partially digestible; Maltrin = fully digestible; Olean = indigestible. |
| 11 | Why is the statement "Vitamins A, D, E and K have been added" needed on products made with olestra? | Because olestra inhibits the absorption of these fat-soluble vitamins, carrying them out of the body as it passes through undigested. |
| 12 | Which olestra-containing products are commercially available in Canada? | None of them. Olestra is not approved in Canada, so no potato chips, low-fat yoghurt or processed meats made with it are sold here. |
| 13 | Under Canada's Food and Drug Regulations, what are sweetening AGENTS and which Division covers them? | Sweetening agents are the real sugars: honey, sugar and molasses. They are covered by Division 18. |
| 14 | Under Canada's Food and Drug Regulations, what are sweetenERS and which Division covers them? | Sweeteners are the sugar substitutes, regulated as food additives. They are covered by Division 16. |
| 15 | Sugar substitutes fall into which two categories, based on digestibility? | Digestible = low caloric (aspartame, sorbitol); indigestible = non-caloric (cyclamate, sucralose, acesulfame-K). |
| 16 | Aspartame: caloric class, composition, relative sweetness, and required warning (and why)? | Low caloric (digestible); made of two amino acids; about 200x sweeter than sucrose; carries a PKU warning because people with phenylketonuria cannot metabolize phenylalanine. It is not heat stable, so it breaks down in baking. |
| 17 | Cyclamate: caloric class and where you would encounter it? | Non-caloric (indigestible) sweetener; it is the sweetener in Sweet'N Low sold in Canada. |
| 18 | Sorbitol: caloric class, chemical family, sweetness, and signature property? | Low caloric (digestible) sugar alcohol that is LESS sweet than sucrose (sweetness index about 50). It gives a cooling sensation. |
| 19 | Sucralose: caloric class, relative sweetness, and heat stability? | Non-caloric (indigestible), about 600x sweeter than sucrose, and heat stable. It is the sweetener in Splenda, so it can be used in baking. |
| 20 | Acesulfame-K: caloric class, relative sweetness, and heat stability? | Non-caloric (indigestible), about 200x sweeter than sucrose, and heat stable. It is often blended with other sweeteners. |
| 21 | Which sugar substitute provides a cool-refreshing sensation in chewing gum, and what is the mechanism? | Sorbitol. Sugar alcohols absorb heat (endothermic) as they dissolve in saliva, so the mouth feels cool. |
| 22 | Name the two plant-derived "natural" sweeteners covered in Lesson 3. | Stevia (from a plant leaf) and monk fruit. |
| 23 | On the relative sweetness index, what is the reference value, and what are the values for fructose, glucose, maltose, lactose, sorbitol and xylitol? | Sucrose = 100 is the reference. Fructose 140, glucose (dextrose) 70-80, maltose 20, lactose 10-20, sorbitol 50, xylitol 100. |
| 24 | What is the enzymatic sequence that converts starch into progressively sweeter sugars? | Starch (amylose, not sweet) --amylase or acid--> maltose (slightly sweet) --maltase--> glucose (sweet) --glucose isomerase--> fructose (very sweet). |
| 25 | How does the Food and Drug Regulations table of "Food Additives That May Be Used as Sweeteners" (Table IX) control each sweetener? | It lists each additive with the foods it is permitted in or on and the maximum level of use. Example: acesulfame-potassium is allowed at 0.35% in chewing gum and 0.025% in carbonated beverages as consumed. |
| 26 | What are the three sensory factors that determine whether a food is good (yummy)? | Appearance, texture and flavour — where flavour includes aroma and taste. |
| 27 | Food texture is measured as which four mechanical forces? | Compression, cutting, shearing and tensile strength. |
| 28 | How do we describe the force needed to squeeze a loaf of bread? | Compression. |
| 29 | How do we perceive aroma? | Fat-soluble volatiles interacting with receptors in our nasal passages. |
| 30 | How do we perceive taste, and what are the tastes? | Water-soluble compounds interacting with receptors on the tongue (taste buds). Four classic tastes — sweet, sour, salty, bitter — plus the fifth, umami (savoury). |
| 31 | What is MSG and what does it do? | Monosodium glutamate, a flavour enhancer. It enhances savoury (umami) character and suppresses bitterness. |
| 32 | Match the oral sensations astringency, pungency and coolness with the molecules responsible. | Astringency = tannins; pungency = capsaicin; coolness = xylitol. |
| 33 | Which oral sensation is caused by tannins, and where would you encounter it? | Astringency — the dry, puckery mouthfeel of red wine or strong tea. |
| 34 | Name the three common sensory evaluation tests and what each one measures. | Hedonic test (consumers, how much they like it); discriminative test, a.k.a. difference test (can two products be told apart); descriptive test (trained panel, detailed product profile). |
| 35 | Which sensory test measures consumer acceptance level of a new product, and which offers the most details during product development? | Consumer acceptance level = hedonic test. Most details during product development = descriptive analysis. |

## Cloze facts

- Fat provides {{c1::9}} Cal per gram, and fat substitutes are grouped as {{c2::protein}}-based, {{c2::carbohydrate}}-based or {{c2::fat}}-based, with examples {{c3::Simplesse}}, {{c3::Maltrin}} and {{c3::Olean}} respectively.
- Digestibility of fat substitutes: Simplesse is {{c1::partially digestible}}, Maltrin is {{c2::fully digestible}}, and Olean is {{c3::indigestible}}.
- Simplesse is made from {{c1::soy, milk or egg white protein}} by a process called {{c2::microparticulation}} that forms tiny {{c2::spherical}} particles, and it may be declared in the ingredient list as {{c3::modified protein}}.
- Olestra products must state "Vitamins {{c1::A, D, E and K}} have been added" because olestra {{c2::inhibits the absorption}} of these fat-soluble vitamins; the number of olestra-containing products commercially available in Canada is {{c3::none}}.
- Under the Food and Drug Regulations, sweetening AGENTS (honey, sugar, molasses) fall under Division {{c1::18}}, while sweetenERS, which are {{c2::food additives}}, fall under Division {{c3::16}}.
- Relative sweetness: aspartame is about {{c1::200}}x sucrose, sucralose about {{c2::600}}x, and acesulfame-K about {{c3::200}}x.
- Digestible-low caloric sweeteners are {{c1::aspartame}} and {{c1::sorbitol}}; indigestible-non caloric sweeteners are {{c2::cyclamate}}, {{c2::sucralose}} and {{c2::acesulfame-K}}; the one carrying a PKU warning is {{c3::aspartame}} and the one giving a cooling sensation in gum is {{c3::sorbitol}}.
- On the sweetness index, sucrose = {{c1::100}}, fructose = {{c2::140}}, glucose = {{c2::70-80}}, maltose = {{c3::20}}, sorbitol = {{c3::50}} and xylitol = {{c3::100}}.
- Sweetness sequence: starch (amylose, not sweet) is broken down by {{c1::amylase or acid}} to maltose (slightly sweet), then by {{c2::maltase}} to glucose (sweet), then by {{c3::glucose isomerase}} to fructose (very sweet).
- The three sensory factors are {{c1::appearance}}, {{c1::texture}} and {{c1::flavour}}, where flavour includes {{c2::aroma}} and {{c2::taste}}; texture is measured as the mechanical forces {{c3::compression, cutting, shearing and tensile strength}}.
- Aroma is perceived as {{c1::fat}}-soluble volatiles interacting with receptors in our {{c1::nasal passages}}, while taste is {{c2::water}}-soluble compounds on the tongue; the four tastes are sweet, sour, salty and bitter, plus the fifth, {{c3::umami}}, whose enhancer MSG ({{c3::monosodium glutamate}}) boosts savouriness and suppresses {{c3::bitterness}}.
- Oral sensations: astringency is caused by {{c1::tannins}}, pungency by {{c1::capsaicin}} and coolness by {{c1::xylitol}}; the three common sensory tests are the {{c2::hedonic}} test (consumer acceptance), the {{c2::discriminative}} or difference test (products), and the {{c3::descriptive}} test, which offers the most details during product development.

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 03 Practice MCQs
### Fat and Sugar Substitutes; Sensory Perception of Foods

---

### Q1. Which of the following statements is true for Simplesse?

A. The fat used to make deep-fried products, such as French fries, can be replaced by Simplesse.
B. Microparticulation is a process used to make tiny spherical Simplesse particles.
C. Products made with Simplesse must be labelled with "Simplesse" in the ingredient list.
D. Simplesse is a carbohydrate-based fat substitute.
E. Simplesse is a fat substitute because it is indigestible.

**Answer: B** — Simplesse is made by microparticulation into tiny spherical protein particles. The others are the classic corrections: Olean (not Simplesse) can replace deep-frying fat, Simplesse may be listed as "modified protein," it is protein-based, and it is partially digestible.

---

### Q2. Which of the following pairings of fat substitute and digestibility are correct?

(i) Simplesse — partially digestible
(ii) Maltrin — indigestible
(iii) Olean — indigestible
(iv) Maltrin — fully digestible
(v) Simplesse — fully digestible

A. (i), (ii), (v)
B. (i), (iii), (iv)
C. (ii), (iii), (iv)
D. (iii), (iv), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — The Lesson 3 matching set is Simplesse partially digestible, Maltrin (maltodextrin) fully digestible, and Olean indigestible.

---

### Q3. The fat used to make deep-fried products, such as French fries and potato chips, can be replaced by which fat substitute?

A. Simplesse
B. Maltrin
C. Olean
D. All of the above
E. None of the above

**Answer: C** — Olean is fat-based and heat stable, so it survives deep-frying. Simplesse is protein-based and Maltrin is carbohydrate-based; neither can replace deep-frying fat.

---

### Q4. Why is the statement "Vitamins A, D, E and K have been added" needed on products made with Olestra?

A. Enzymes that digest Olestra may also digest these vitamins.
B. Olestra inhibits the absorption of these vitamins.
C. Products made with Olestra are usually deep fried; deep frying destroys the availability of these vitamins.
D. There is a need to enhance vitamin contents in potato chips.
E. All of the above.

**Answer: B** — Olestra passes through the gut undigested and carries the fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E and K out with it, inhibiting their absorption. Note that A is wrong precisely because Olestra is not digested by enzymes at all.

---

### Q5. Which of the following Olestra-containing products is commercially available in Canada?

A. Low fat yoghurt
B. Processed meats
C. Potato chips
D. All of the above
E. None of the above

**Answer: E** — Olestra is not approved in Canada, so none of these products is commercially available here. "Fat free" Pringles made with Olestra were a US product.

---

### Q6. Which of the following are classified as sweetening agents under Division 18 of the Food and Drug Regulations?

(i) Honey
(ii) Sucralose
(iii) Sugar
(iv) Molasses
(v) Aspartame

A. (i), (ii), (iii)
B. (i), (iii), (iv)
C. (ii), (iv), (v)
D. (iii), (iv), (v)
E. (i), (ii), (iv)

**Answer: B** — Division 18 covers sweetening agents: honey, sugar and molasses. Sucralose and aspartame are sweeteners, i.e., food additives regulated under Division 16.

---

### Q7. Products containing which sweetener must carry a warning for people with phenylketonuria (PKU)?

A. Aspartame
B. Cyclamate
C. Sorbitol
D. Sucralose
E. Acesulfame-K

**Answer: A** — Aspartame is made of two amino acids, one of which is phenylalanine; people with PKU cannot metabolize it.

---

### Q8. Which of the following fat or sugar substitutes are heat stable?

(i) Aspartame
(ii) Sucralose
(iii) Acesulfame-K
(iv) Simplesse
(v) Olean

A. (i), (ii), (iii)
B. (i), (iv), (v)
C. (ii), (iii), (v)
D. (ii), (iv), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — Sucralose and acesulfame-K are the heat-stable sweeteners, and Olean is heat stable enough to deep-fry with. Aspartame breaks down with heat and Simplesse is a protein that cannot withstand frying temperatures.

---

### Q9. Which of the following sugar substitutes are indigestible and therefore non-caloric?

(i) Aspartame
(ii) Cyclamate
(iii) Sorbitol
(iv) Sucralose
(v) Acesulfame-K

A. (i), (ii), (iii)
B. (i), (iii), (iv)
C. (ii), (iii), (v)
D. (ii), (iv), (v)
E. (iii), (iv), (v)

**Answer: D** — Cyclamate, sucralose and acesulfame-K are indigestible and non-caloric. Aspartame and sorbitol are digestible and therefore low caloric, not non-caloric.

---

### Q10. Which sugar substitute provides a cool-refreshing sensation in chewing gum?

A. Aspartame
B. Cyclamate
C. Sorbitol
D. Sucralose
E. Acesulfame-K

**Answer: C** — Sorbitol is a sugar alcohol; sugar alcohols absorb heat as they dissolve in saliva, producing the cooling sensation. (Xylitol, the other sugar alcohol in this lesson, is the molecule credited with "coolness" as an oral sensation.)

---

### Q11. How do we describe the force needed to squeeze a loaf of bread?

A. Compression
B. Cutting
C. Shearing
D. Tensile strength
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — Squeezing is compression. Cutting, shearing and tensile strength (pulling apart) are the other mechanical forces used to measure food texture.

---

### Q12. How do we perceive aroma?

A. Fat soluble volatiles interacting with receptors in our nasal passages
B. Fat soluble volatiles interacting with receptors on our tongue
C. Water soluble volatiles interacting with receptors in our nasal passages
D. Water soluble volatiles interacting with receptors on our tongue
E. Water soluble non-volatiles interacting with receptors in our nasal passages

**Answer: A** — Aroma is fat-soluble volatiles reaching receptors in the nasal passages; the water-soluble, tongue-based half of flavour is taste.

---

### Q13. Which of the following is true of MSG?

A. It is monosodium glutamate.
B. It acts as a flavour enhancer.
C. It enhances the savoury (umami) character of foods.
D. It suppresses bitterness.
E. All of the above.

**Answer: E** — MSG is monosodium glutamate, a flavour enhancer that adds savouriness (umami, the confirmed fifth taste) and suppresses bitterness.

---

### Q14. Match the oral sensations with the responsible molecules.

(i) Astringency
(ii) Pungency
(iii) Coolness

(a) Xylitol
(b) Capsaicin
(c) Tannins

A. (i)-(a), (ii)-(b), (iii)-(c)
B. (i)-(a), (ii)-(c), (iii)-(b)
C. (i)-(b), (ii)-(a), (iii)-(c)
D. (i)-(b), (ii)-(c), (iii)-(a)
E. (i)-(c), (ii)-(b), (iii)-(a)

**Answer: E** — Tannins cause the dry, puckery astringency, capsaicin causes pungency (burn), and xylitol causes coolness.

---

### Q15. A company reformulates its cola with a new sweetener. It first wants to know whether consumers can tell the new version from the original, then how much consumers like it, and finally wants a trained panel to profile every attribute in detail. In order, the appropriate tests are:

A. Hedonic test, discriminative test, descriptive analysis
B. Discriminative test, hedonic test, descriptive analysis
C. Descriptive analysis, hedonic test, discriminative test
D. Discriminative test, descriptive analysis, hedonic test
E. Hedonic test, descriptive analysis, discriminative test

**Answer: B** — The discriminative (difference) test asks whether two products can be told apart, the hedonic test measures consumer acceptance/liking, and descriptive analysis by a trained panel offers the most details during product development.
