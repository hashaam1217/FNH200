## Flashcards

| # | Question | Answer |

| 1 | List the benefits (advantages) of food processing. | Food safety; year-round availability regardless of season or climate; longer shelf life; convenience; less waste; and improved bioavailability of nutrients. |
| 2 | List the risks (disadvantages) of food processing, and the framing caveat about raw foods. | Risks: loss of some nutrients (especially heat- and water-sensitive vitamins) and changes in flavour, colour and texture. Caveat: raw foods vary widely in nutrient content (genetics, climate, soil, ripeness) sometimes as much as or more than processing changes them. |
| 3 | Deck quiz: Processing increases the bioavailability of nutrients by destroying which of the following? (amylase inhibitors / thiaminase / avidin) | All of the above. Heat processing destroys all three antinutritional (antidigestive) factors. |
| 4 | Where are trypsin and amylase inhibitors found, and what does heat processing do to them? | Found in cereal grains, peas and beans. Heat destroys these antidigestive factors, improving the bioavailability (digestibility) of the proteins and carbohydrates in these products. |
| 5 | What is thiaminase, where is it found, and why does heat processing matter? | An enzyme that destroys thiamin (vitamin B1); found in fish, shellfish, brussels sprouts and red cabbage. Heat processing destroys it, sparing the thiamin. |
| 6 | What does avidin do, where is it found, and what destroys it? | Avidin (with other factors in raw egg white) binds biotin and some of the iron present in egg yolk, making these nutrients biologically unavailable. Heat processing destroys it. |
| 7 | Besides destroying antinutritional factors, name the other positive nutritional effects of heat processing. | Increases digestibility of starch (gelatinization) and protein (denaturation); increases bioavailability of niacin, which is present in many cereals in a bound form; increases palatability, giving greater appeal and nutrient consumption. |
| 8 | Deck quiz: Which nutrient is MOST stable under thermal processing - carbohydrates, protein, fat, vitamins or minerals? | Minerals. Vitamins are the fragile group; minerals can leach into cooking water but do not degrade. |
| 9 | Deck quiz: Which vitamin is LEAST stable at normal food pH (below pH 7) - A, C or E? | Vitamin A. In Table 2 vitamin A is unstable in acid, while vitamins C and E are stable in acid. |
| 10 | Table 2: give the full stability profile of vitamin C. | Unstable at neutral pH, in alkali, in air/oxygen, in light and in heat. Stable only in acid (pH below 7). |
| 11 | Table 2: give the stability profile of vitamin A and beta-carotene. | Both are unstable in acid, in air/oxygen, in light and in heat. Both are stable at neutral pH. |
| 12 | Table 2: give the stability profiles of vitamin E and riboflavin. | Vitamin E is unstable in air/oxygen, light and heat (stable across pH). Riboflavin is notably unstable in light - which is why milk is not sold in clear glass. |
| 13 | Table 2: give the stability profile of thiamin (B1). | Unstable in alkali and in heat; stable in acid. It is the weakest of the thiamin/niacin/pyridoxine trio. |
| 14 | Learning objective: compare and contrast the stability of (a) vitamins A, C and E versus (b) thiamin (B1), niacin (B2) and pyridoxine (B6). | A, C and E are the vulnerable group - readily destroyed by oxygen, light and heat (plus acid for A, and everything but acid for C). Thiamin, niacin and pyridoxine are the comparatively stable group, with niacin the most stable and thiamin the weakest of the three. |
| 15 | Table 3: percentage nutrient losses from blanching in water versus with steam, and why they differ. | Water: vitamin C 16-58%, riboflavin 30-50%, thiamin 16-34%, niacin 32-37%. Steam: vitamin C only 16-26%, vitamin B6 21% - steam retains more because water-soluble vitamins leach out into the blanching water. |
| 16 | Table 4: percentage nutrient losses from conventional canning of vegetables. | Vitamin C 33-90%; thiamin 16-83%; riboflavin 25-67%; niacin 0-75%; folacin 35-84%; pantothenic acid 30-85%; vitamin B6 0-91%; biotin 0-78%; vitamin A 0-84%. |
| 17 | Table 5: thiamin losses for aseptic HTST versus conventional thermal processing. | Strained lima beans 15.8% (HTST) vs 40.3% (conventional); strained beef 9.5% vs 21.6%; tomato juice concentrate 0% vs 2.8%. High-temperature/short-time processing spares more thiamin. |
| 18 | Table 5: pyridoxine losses for HTST versus conventional - what is the nuance? | Strained lima beans 9.5% vs 10.1%; strained beef 4.1% vs 2.9%; tomato juice concentrate 0% vs 0%. Pyridoxine differences are small, and for strained beef HTST was actually slightly higher - the big HTST advantage is in thiamin. |
| 19 | Deck quiz: Which processing leads to the MOST loss of vitamin C? | Commercial sterilization (conventional canning) - up to 90% loss per Table 4. Not blanching, not HTST, not UHT. |
| 20 | Deck quiz: Which processing leads to the LEAST nutrient loss - pasteurization, UHT, refrigeration, freezing or irradiation? | Freezing. Newsome: freezing "results in a nutritive quality closest to that of fresh, raw foods," with nutrient loss negligible under proper packaging and processing conditions. |
| 21 | What are the exceptions to "negligible" nutrient loss during freezing? | Small losses of vitamin C and other water-soluble vitamins in vegetables and fruits during the blanching step beforehand, and small (10-20%) unexplainable losses of water-soluble vitamins in pork. |
| 22 | What freezing conditions are required for good nutrient retention? | Freeze quickly and solidly, use air-impermeable packaging, and maintain a constant low temperature (-18 degrees F or lower). Foods not frozen this way, or that undergo intermittent thawing, show greater drip loss on thawing and hence greater nutrient loss. |
| 23 | Why is the shelf life of frozen foods (9 months to 1 year) shorter than that of canned foods? | Because not all of the water in food freezes, and some fatty acids as well as vitamins A, C and E tend to oxidize during storage when exposed to air. Chemical changes therefore continue even in the frozen state. |
| 24 | Deck quiz: Name the process also known as "cold sterilization." | Irradiation. It sterilizes without heat - not commercial sterilization, IQF, freeze drying, or low-temperature fermentation. |
| 25 | Deck quiz: Which technique should NOT be used to minimize nutrient loss during storage, and which ones should? | Should NOT: storage at high temperature. Should: storage at low temperature, addition of antioxidants, protective coatings, vacuum packaging, and packaging in tin foil. |
| 26 | What does Newsome say about storage temperature for canned goods, and about "garden-fresh" versus "market-fresh"? | Canned goods stored up to one year below 70-75 degrees F show less than a 10-20% change in vitamin concentration, but storage above room temperature markedly decreases retention (e.g. canned tomato juice). Foods processed under GMP from freshly harvested "garden-fresh" commodities frequently have higher nutrient content than "market-fresh" produce mishandled or stored during transport. |
| 27 | List at least five strategies to minimize nutrient loss when preparing foods at home. | Do not overcook; steam or microwave instead of boiling; use minimal water; keep the cooking liquid for soups and sauces; leave skins on where possible; cut into larger pieces (less surface area); cook just before serving rather than reheating repeatedly; store produce cold and covered. |
| 28 | Compare and contrast restoration, fortification and enrichment. | Restoration = adding back nutrients lost during processing. | 29 | Deck examples: classify orange juice with added calcium and vitamin D, apple juice labelled "100% vitamin C", and Five Roses white flour marked "ENRICHED." | OJ with calcium and vitamin D = FORTIFIED (not naturally present). Apple juice "100% vitamin C" = RESTORED (naturally present, lost in processing). Five Roses white flour = ENRICHED (nutrients removed by milling added back to a regulated standard). |

(Deck notes the detailed fortification slides are not on the final exam.) |
| 31 | Lasagna sample exam question: how does cooking affect the SPINACH component (vitamin C)? | At pH above 4.6 (near neutral) vitamin C is unstable, and it is also destroyed by heat and oxygen, so there is significant loss. Loss is worse if the spinach is boiled in a lot of water, since vitamin C leaches out. |
| 32 | Lasagna sample exam question: how does cooking affect the TOMATO component (vitamin A)? | Tomato is acidic, and Table 2 shows vitamin A is unstable in acid as well as in heat, oxygen and light. Expect losses of vitamin A. |
| 33 | Lasagna sample exam question: how does cooking affect the BEEF component (fat and protein)? | Fat can render out of the meat and is subject to oxidation. Protein denatures with heat, which actually improves its digestibility - a nutritional gain, not a loss. |
| 34 | Lasagna sample exam question: how does cooking affect the CHEESE (calcium) and the PASTA (carbohydrate and thiamin)? | Cheese calcium is a mineral, so it is stable - no loss. Pasta: cooking gelatinizes the starch and destroys amylase (digestive) inhibitors, improving carbohydrate bioavailability, while heat-sensitive thiamin is degraded and leaches into the cooking water. |
| 35 | Lasagna sample exam question: how should you structure each component of your answer, and what changes if the lasagna is FROZEN rather than cooked? | Structure: name the nutrient, state its stability from Table 2 (pH, oxygen, light, heat), then say what the process does to it. ## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 11: Nutrient Retention — Practice MCQs

Source: Lesson 11 lecture deck (2026S) + Newsome, R.L. 1986, "Effects of Food Processing on Nutritive Values," *Food Technology* 40(12):109–116.

### Q1. Which nutrient below is MOST stable under thermal processing?
A. Carbohydrates
B. Protein
C. Fat
D. Vitamins
E. Minerals

**Answer: E** — Minerals are elements and do not degrade with heat; they can only leach into cooking water. Vitamins are the fragile group.

### Q2. Processing increases the bioavailability of nutrients by destroying:
A. Amylase inhibitors
B. Thiaminase
C. Avidin
D. All of the above
E. None of the above

**Answer: D** — Heat destroys amylase/trypsin inhibitors in cereal grains, peas and beans; thiaminase in fish, shellfish, brussels sprouts and red cabbage; and avidin in raw egg white.

### Q3. Which vitamin below is LEAST stable at normal food pH (< 7)?
A. Vitamin A
B. Vitamin C
C. Vitamin E
D. Riboflavin
E. Niacin

**Answer: A** — Table 2 marks vitamin A as unstable in acid, whereas C, E, riboflavin and niacin are all stable in acid. Vitamin C is the fragile one *generally* (neutral pH, alkali, oxygen, light, heat) but not in acid.

### Q4. Which processing below will lead to the MOST loss of vitamin C?
A. Blanching in water
B. Blanching with steam
C. Commercial sterilization
D. High temperature, short time process
E. Ultra high temperature process

**Answer: C** — Table 4 gives 33–90% vitamin C loss for conventional canning (commercial sterilization), versus 16–58% for water blanching and 16–26% for steam blanching.

### Q5. Which processing below leads to the LEAST nutrient loss?
A. Pasteurization
B. Ultra high temperature processing
C. Refrigeration
D. Freezing
E. Irradiation

**Answer: D** — Newsome states freezing "results in a nutritive quality closest to that of fresh, raw foods," with negligible loss under proper packaging and processing conditions.

### Q6. Name the process that is also known as "cold sterilization."
A. Commercial sterilization
B. Individual quick frozen
C. Freeze drying
D. Fermentation at low temperature
E. Irradiation

**Answer: E** — Irradiation achieves sterilization without heat, hence the term "cold sterilization."

### Q7. Which technique below should NOT be used to minimize nutrient loss during storage?
A. Storage at low temperature
B. Storage at high temperature
C. Addition of antioxidants
D. Use of protective coatings
E. Vacuum packaging

**Answer: B** — All the others are recommended (as is packaging in tin foil). Storage above room temperature markedly decreases vitamin retention; below 70–75 °F for up to a year keeps losses under 10–20%.

### Q8. Which of the following are listed as BENEFITS of food processing?
(i) Year-round availability regardless of season or climate
(ii) Changes in flavour, colour and texture
(iii) Increased bioavailability of nutrients
(iv) Reduced food waste
(v) Loss of heat- and water-sensitive vitamins

A. (i), (iii), (iv)
B. (i), (ii), (iv)
C. (ii), (v)
D. (i), (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — Items (ii) and (v) are the risks/disadvantages side of the ledger, not benefits.

### Q9. According to Table 2, which of the following are UNSTABLE in acid (pH < 7)?
(i) Vitamin A
(ii) Vitamin C
(iii) Beta-carotene
(iv) Vitamin E
(v) Mineral salts

A. (i), (iii)
B. (i), (ii)
C. (ii), (iv)
D. (i), (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — Vitamin A and beta-carotene are unstable in acid; vitamin C, vitamin E and mineral salts are all stable in acid.

### Q10. According to the article, orange juice sold with added calcium and vitamin D has been…
A. Restored
B. Fortified
C. Enriched
D. All of the above
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — Calcium and vitamin D are not naturally present in orange juice, so adding them is fortification (not putting back a processing loss, and not to a legal standard).

### Q11. Apple juice labelled "100% Vitamin C" has been…
A. Restored
B. Fortified
C. Enriched
D. Both restored and enriched
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — Vitamin C occurs naturally in the fruit and is lost during processing, so adding it back is restoration. Contrast Five Roses white flour, which is *enriched* because milling losses are replaced to a regulated standard.

### Q12. In Table 5, the thiamin loss in strained lima beans processed by aseptic HTST was:
A. 9.5%
B. 15.8%
C. 21.6%
D. 40.3%
E. 2.8%

**Answer: B** — HTST gave 15.8% thiamin loss versus 40.3% for conventional thermal processing; 9.5% and 21.6% are the strained beef pair, and 2.8% is conventional tomato juice concentrate.

### Q13. Which of the following help retain nutrients in frozen foods?
(i) Freezing quickly and solidly
(ii) Holding at a constant low temperature (−18 °F or lower)
(iii) Allowing intermittent thawing between storage periods
(iv) Using air-impermeable packaging
(v) Storing above room temperature before freezing

A. (i), (ii), (iv)
B. (i), (iii)
C. (ii), (iv), (v)
D. (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — Intermittent thawing causes greater-than-normal drip loss and therefore greater nutrient loss; high storage temperatures degrade vitamins.

### Q14. Which of the following are recommended strategies to minimize nutrient loss when preparing foods at home?
A. Use minimal cooking water
B. Steam or microwave instead of boiling
C. Save the cooking liquid for soups and sauces
D. Leave skins on and cut produce into larger pieces
E. All of the above

**Answer: E** — All are on the home-preparation list, together with not overcooking and cooking just before serving rather than reheating repeatedly.

### Q15. In the lasagna sample exam question, which component's key nutrient is essentially UNAFFECTED by baking?
A. Spinach — vitamin C
B. Tomato — vitamin A
C. Cheese — calcium
D. Pasta — thiamin
E. Beef — fat

**Answer: C** — Calcium is a mineral and therefore stable to heat. Spinach vitamin C is lost at pH > 4.6 with heat and leaching, tomato vitamin A is unstable in acid and heat, pasta thiamin is heat-labile and leaches out, and beef fat can render out and oxidize.
