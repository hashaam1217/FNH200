## Cloze facts

- {{c1::Japan}} led the way in regulating functional foods with a system called {{c2::FOSHU}} (Food for Specific Health Use); Canada's parallel framework is {{c3::Natural and Non-Prescription Health Products}}.
- The Natural Health Products Regulations came into effect on {{c1::January 1}}, {{c2::2004}}.
- NHPs are defined as {{c1::probiotics}}, {{c2::herbal remedies}}, {{c3::vitamins and minerals}}, {{c4::homeopathic medicines}}, {{c5::traditional medicines such as traditional Chinese medicines}}, and other products like {{c6::amino acids and essential fatty acids}}; they must be safe as {{c7::over-the-counter}} products, while products needing a prescription are regulated as {{c8::drugs}} under the {{c8::Food and Drug Regulations}}.
- A product is authorized for sale in Canada if it has an {{c1::NPN}} (Natural Product Number) or a {{c2::DIN-HM}} ({{c3::Homeopathic Medicine Number}}), searchable in the {{c4::Licensed Natural Health Product Database}}.
- Four ways to develop functional foods: by adding {{c1::vitamins and/or minerals}}; by adding {{c2::bioactive ingredients}}; increased bioactives through {{c3::breeding and/or genetic modifications}}; increased bioactives through {{c4::processing and/or livestock feeding}}.
- Known benefits of probiotics: reduce {{c1::gastrointestinal discomfort and disorder}}, control {{c2::irritable bowel syndrome}}, and prevent development of {{c3::colon cancer}}.
- Real recognized probiotic species include {{c1::Lactobacillus acidophilus}} and {{c2::Bifidobacterium bifidus}}; by contrast {{c3::Bifidus regularis}} is only a trademarked marketing name and is {{c4::not}} an approved probiotic.
- For a probiotic claim, {{c1::1.0 x 10^9}} units of approved probiotics must be present in each {{c2::serving}}; to have enough for health benefit the slide gives {{c3::1.0 x 10^9}} units per {{c4::mL}} and {{c5::100 mL, twice a week}}.

- For the camping pasta question the best answer is {{c1::dehydration (freeze drying or vacuum microwave drying)}} packaged in a {{c2::strong water barrier such as plastic}} that is {{c3::lightweight}}; explicitly not acceptable are {{c4::pasteurization, freezing, irradiation, cans, and glass}}.

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 13 Practice MCQs (Food, Nutrition, and Health)

Exam-style questions drawn only from the Lesson 13 slide deck and podcast transcript.

---

### Q1. Which country led the way in the regulation of functional foods?

A. Canada
B. China
C. Japan
D. Korea
E. The United States

**Answer: C** — Japan led with FOSHU (Food for Specific Health Use). Canada's parallel framework is Natural and Non-Prescription Health Products.

---

### Q2. A bottle of Calcium Cal-Chews chewable tablets labelled "Strengthens Bones" is an example of...

A. Functional Foods
B. Natural Health Product
C. Nutraceuticals
D. Nutritionals
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — The dosage form is a chewable tablet/supplement, so it is a natural health product, not a functional food. The format decides the category, not the calcium itself.

---

### Q3. A carton of Minute Maid Premium orange juice with added Calcium and Vitamin D is an example of...

A. Functional Foods
B. Natural Health Product
C. Nutraceuticals
D. Nutritionals
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — It is still a food/beverage that delivers a health benefit beyond basic nutrition, so it is a functional food. The same calcium in a tablet would instead be an NHP.

---

### Q4. What are natural health products?

(i) Vitamins, minerals, amino acids, and fatty acids
(ii) Herbal remedies
(iii) Homeopathic medicines
(iv) Traditional medicines from different cultures
(v) Probiotics

A. (i)
B. (ii), (iii)
C. (iii), (iv)
D. (ii), (iii), and (iv)
E. All

**Answer: E** — Under the Natural Health Products Regulations all five categories are NHPs, along with other products like essential fatty acids. This is the deck's own quiz slide.

---

### Q5. Which of the following are examples of functional foods?

(i) Benecol cholesterol-lowering light spread
(ii) Oat BG22 oat bran powder with 20% beta-glucan
(iii) Green tea with ginseng
(iv) Probiotic yogurt
(v) Plain raw carrots

A. (i)
B. (i) and (ii)
C. (i), (ii), (iii)
D. (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv)
E. All of them

**Answer: E** — Carrots count too, because functional foods include foods whose bioactives (beta-carotene) are naturally present; nothing has to be added.

---

### Q6. How do we know a bottle of Jamieson Canadian Ginseng has been authorized for sale in Canada?

A. It has "Canadian" on the label
B. It has an approved health claim on the label
C. The manufacturer is reliable
D. The manufacturer has been in operation since 1922
E. It has an NPN (or DIN-HM) number

**Answer: E** — Only a Natural Product Number or Homeopathic Medicine Number shows Health Canada authorization; these are searchable in the Licensed Natural Health Product Database. "Canadian" branding and a health claim on the package prove nothing.

---

### Q7. The Natural Health Products Regulations came into effect on:

A. January 1, 1994
B. January 1, 2004
C. August 21, 2004
D. January 1, 2014
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — The NHP Regulations took effect January 1, 2004. Products that require a prescription are excluded and regulated as drugs under the Food and Drug Regulations.

---

### Q8. Which of the following is NOT listed for a product in the Licensed Natural Health Product Database?

A. Product licence holder
B. NPN or DIN-HM number
C. Medicinal and non-medicinal ingredients
D. The product's retail price and where to buy it
E. Risk information such as cautions, warnings, contra-indications and known adverse reactions

**Answer: D** — The database lists product name, licence holder, NPN/DIN-HM, medicinal and non-medicinal ingredients, dosage form, recommended use or purpose (the health claim), and risk information. Price and retailer are not part of it.

---

### Q9. Which of the following are recognized ways to develop functional foods?

(i) Adding vitamins and/or minerals
(ii) Adding bioactive ingredients
(iii) Producing foods with increased bioactives through breeding and/or genetic modification
(iv) Producing foods with increased bioactives through processing and/or livestock feeding
(v) Removing all naturally occurring bioactive compounds from the food

A. (i), (ii)
B. (i), (ii), (iii)
C. (ii), (iii), (iv)
D. (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv)
E. All of them

**Answer: D** — The deck lists exactly four routes; (v) is not one of them. Omega-3 eggs from flaxseed-fed hens are the classic example of route (iv).

---

### Q10. Is "Bifidus regularis," advertised in large letters on Activia yoghurt, an approved health-promoting probiotic?

A. Yes — it is approved as a probiotic under the Natural Health Products Regulations
B. Yes — it appears in the Licensed Natural Health Product Database with its own NPN
C. No — it is a trademarked marketing name, not an approved probiotic designation
D. No — it is an approved probiotic in Japan under FOSHU but not in Canada
E. None of the above

**Answer: C** — "Bifidus regularis" is branding that is made to look like a species name. The real, recognized probiotic species named in the course are *Lactobacillus acidophilus* and *Bifidobacterium bifidus*.

---

### Q11. What is the minimum level of probiotics needed for a product to make a probiotic claim?

A. 1.0 x 10^3 units of approved probiotics per serving
B. 1.0 x 10^6 units of approved probiotics per serving
C. 1.0 x 10^9 units of approved probiotics per serving
D. 1.0 x 10^9 units of approved probiotics per package, regardless of serving size
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — The slide states 1.0 x 10^9 units of approved probiotics must be present in each serving of product. To actually obtain the health benefit the deck adds 1.0 x 10^9 units per mL, 100 mL twice a week.

---

### Q12. Which of the following are listed as known benefits of probiotics?

(i) Reduce gastrointestinal discomfort and disorder
(ii) Control irritable bowel syndrome
(iii) Prevent development of colon cancer
(iv) Replace the need for an NPN on a supplement label
(v) Provide benefit even when the organisms are no longer viable

A. (i), (ii), (iii)
B. (i), (iii)
C. (ii), (iii), (iv)
D. (i), (ii), (iii), and (v)
E. All of them

**Answer: A** — Only the first three are listed as known benefits, with many more still under investigation. The bacteria must be viable (alive) and survive distribution, retailing and storage in the consumer's home, so (v) is wrong.

---

### Q13. A camping supply company is developing a quality pasta in tomato sauce for multi-day hikers. Which preservation technique earns full marks on the sample short-answer question?

A. Pasteurization
B. Freezing
C. Irradiation
D. Dehydration by freeze drying or vacuum microwave drying
E. All of the above

**Answer: D** — The vacuum removes water at a lower temperature, preventing quality loss; the dried product is lighter to carry and rehydrates easily because of the void (puff) structure. Pasteurization, freezing, irradiation, cans and glass are explicitly listed as not acceptable.

---

### Q14. For that same camping pasta question, which packaging answers would receive credit?

(i) A strong water barrier such as plastic
(ii) Lightweight material suited to a hiker
(iii) A retort pouch pre-sterilized with hydrogen peroxide
(iv) A glass jar
(v) A metal can

A. (i), (ii)
B. (i), (ii), (iii)
C. (i), (iii), (iv)
D. (ii), (iv), (v)
E. All of them

**Answer: B** — Plastic water-barrier film and lightweight construction fit the freeze-dried best answer, and the retort pouch fits the partial-credit commercial-sterilization answer. Cans and glass are explicitly not acceptable.

---

### Q15. On the green-potato short-answer question, part (c) asks "What would you do? Explain. (1 mark)". What does the marking scheme require to earn the mark?

A. A balanced discussion of both options without committing to one
B. Declaring a position (yes or no) and then explaining it
C. Simply stating that solanine cannot be destroyed by heat
D. Recommending that the green portions be trimmed off before serving
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — The slide says in bold that you must DECLARE a position, yes or no, then explain, to earn the mark. Judy's model answer is that she would not bring the potato salad, because grandpa is elderly with a weak immune system and will eat a lot of it.
