## Cloze facts

- The eight causes of food deterioration are microorganisms, {{c1::food enzymes and other chemical reactions}}, reaction with {{c1::oxygen and/or light}}, {{c2::gain or loss of moisture}}, {{c2::inappropriate temperatures}}, {{c3::time}}, {{c3::physical stress or abuse}}, and {{c3::infestation}} by insects, parasites, and rodents.
- Causes of food deterioration are grouped as {{c1::physical}}, {{c2::chemical}}, or {{c3::biological}}, and no method can completely {{c1::eliminate spoilage}}.
- Ranked from least to most resistant in a normal environment: {{c1::vegetative cells of bacteria}}, then {{c2::yeasts}}, then {{c3::moulds}}, then {{c1::bacterial spores}}, whose named example is {{c2::Clostridium botulinum}}.
- By oxygen requirement, microorganisms are {{c1::aerobic}}, {{c2::anaerobic}}, or {{c3::facultative anaerobes}}; the ones able to grow in canned food are {{c2::anaerobes}} and {{c3::facultative anaerobes}}.
- Growth temperature ranges: psychrophiles {{c1::0 to 18}} degrees C, psychrotrophs {{c2::-5 to 35}} degrees C, mesophiles {{c3::10 to 45}} degrees C, thermophiles {{c3::50 to over 100}} degrees C; most spoilage of refrigerated food is caused by {{c1::psychrotrophs}}, not psychrophiles.
- Microorganisms are the GOOD ({{c1::fermentation}}), the BAD ({{c2::spoilage of our foods}}), and the UGLY ({{c3::foodborne illness}}).
- Fermentation can be carried out by {{c1::vegetative cells of bacteria}}, {{c2::yeasts}}, and {{c3::moulds}}, but not by {{c1::bacterial spores}}.
- Browning of a cut apple is caused by the enzyme {{c1::polyphenol oxidase}} reacting with {{c2::oxygen}}, whereas browning inside an uncut apple is caused by {{c3::improper storage temperature}}.
- Enzymatic browning of apple slices can be prevented by dipping in {{c1::hot water}}, dipping in {{c2::water}}, dipping in water with {{c2::lemon juice}}, or applying {{c3::sulfur dioxide}}.
- Retrogradation makes bread tough and dry through {{c1::syneresis}} squeezing water out from between starch molecules, {{c2::moisture loss during storage}}, and exclusion of water previously absorbed during {{c3::gelatinization}}.
- Perishability tiers: {{c1::perishable}} (fresh meat, milk), {{c2::semi-perishable}} (ice cream, cheese, potato chips), and {{c3::shelf stable}} (canned goods, sugar, flour, dehydrated pasta).
- The primary goals of food preservation are to keep food {{c1::safe}}, {{c2::nutritious}}, and of good {{c3::quality}}, while accepting that no method can completely {{c1::eliminate spoilage}}.

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 05: Food Preservation (Causes of Deterioration)
## Practice MCQs

### Q1. Which of the following microorganisms is the MOST resistant in a normal environment?
A. Vegetative cell of bacteria
B. Yeasts
C. Moulds
D. Bacterial spores
E. None of the above

**Answer: D** — Resistance increases from vegetative cells to yeasts to moulds to bacterial spores. Spores such as those of *Clostridium botulinum* are the most resistant.

### Q2. What type(s) of bacteria can grow in canned food?
(i) Aerobic bacteria
(ii) Anaerobic bacteria
(iii) Facultative anaerobic bacteria

A. (i) only
B. (ii) only
C. (i) and (iii)
D. (i), (ii), and (iii)
E. (ii) and (iii)

**Answer: E** — A sealed can contains no oxygen, so aerobes cannot grow. Anaerobes and facultative anaerobes can, which is why *C. botulinum* is the canned-food hazard.

### Q3. Which group of microorganisms is responsible for most spoilage of refrigerated foods?
A. Psychrophiles
B. Psychrotrophs
C. Mesophiles
D. Thermophiles
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — Psychrotrophs grow from -5 to 35 °C, so they grow at refrigeration temperature and rapidly at room temperature. Psychrophiles (0–18 °C) are the classic trap answer.

### Q4. Which pairing of microorganism class and growth temperature range is CORRECT?
A. Psychrophiles: 10 to 45 °C
B. Psychrotrophs: 0 to 18 °C
C. Mesophiles: -5 to 35 °C
D. Thermophiles: 50 to over 100 °C
E. None of the above

**Answer: D** — The correct ranges are psychrophiles 0–18 °C, psychrotrophs -5 to 35 °C, mesophiles 10–45 °C, and thermophiles 50 to over 100 °C.

### Q5. Which of the following may contribute to food fermentation?
(i) Vegetative cells of bacteria
(ii) Yeasts
(iii) Moulds
(iv) Bacterial spores

A. (i) and (ii)
B. (ii) and (iii)
C. (i), (ii), and (iii)
D. (i), (iii), and (iv)
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — Bacteria, yeasts, and moulds all carry out fermentation (the "GOOD" microorganisms). Bacterial spores are dormant survival structures and do not ferment.

### Q6. Example(s) of semi-perishable foods.
(i) Ice cream
(ii) Cheese
(iii) Potato chips
(iv) Dehydrated pasta

A. (i) and (ii)
B. (i) and (iii)
C. (ii) and (iii)
D. (i), (ii), and (iii)
E. All of the above

**Answer: D** — Ice cream, cheese, and potato chips are semi-perishable. Dehydrated pasta is shelf stable, making it the odd one out.

### Q7. Which enzyme is responsible for the brown colour that develops on a freshly cut apple?
A. Amylase
B. Lipase
C. Pectinase
D. Polyphenol oxidase
E. None of the above

**Answer: D** — Polyphenol oxidase oxidizes phenolic compounds into brown pigments once cutting exposes the tissue to oxygen. This is enzymatic browning, distinct from Maillard browning and caramelization.

### Q8. Ways to prevent apple slices from enzymatic browning.
A. Dipping apple slices in hot water
B. Dipping apple slices in water
C. Dipping apple slices in water with lemon juice
D. Applying sulfur dioxide
E. All of the above

**Answer: E** — Each attacks a different requirement: hot water denatures the enzyme, water excludes oxygen, lemon juice lowers pH, and sulfur dioxide chemically inhibits the enzyme.

### Q9. Retrogradation of bread starch leads to tough and dry bread due to...
A. Syneresis as water is being squeezed out in between the starch molecules
B. Moisture loss during storage of bread
C. Exclusion of water that was previously absorbed during gelatinization
D. All of the above
E. None of the above

**Answer: D** — All three statements describe the same staling process caused by starch retrogradation.

### Q10. An apple that has never been cut is found to be brown on the inside. What is the most likely cause?
A. Exposure to excessive sunlight
B. Infestation by parasites
C. Improper storage temperature
D. Reaction of polyphenol oxidase with oxygen at the peel surface
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — Internal browning of an intact apple is a storage injury caused by inappropriate temperature, not by cutting, sunlight, or infestation.

### Q11. Which of the following is NOT listed in the lecture as a cause of food deterioration?
A. Reaction with oxygen and/or light
B. Gain or loss of moisture
C. Physical stress or abuse
D. Infestation by insects, parasites, and rodents
E. None of the above

**Answer: E** — All four are among the eight listed causes, along with microorganisms, the food's own enzymes and chemical reactions, inappropriate temperatures, and time.

### Q12. In the lecture's classification of microorganisms, the "UGLY" microorganisms are those that...
A. Are used to ferment foods
B. Spoil our foods by producing slime, souring, and off-odours
C. Cause foodborne illnesses
D. All of the above
E. None of the above

**Answer: C** — GOOD = fermentation, BAD = spoilage, UGLY = foodborne illness. The UGLY are especially dangerous because they often do not change the taste or smell of the food.

### Q13. Which of the following statements are TRUE?
(i) Bacterial spores are able to carry out fermentation
(ii) Moulds tolerate a wide range of pH and water activity
(iii) Aerobic bacteria are the main microbial hazard in sealed cans
(iv) *Clostridium botulinum* is an example of a spore-forming bacterium
(v) No preservation method can completely eliminate spoilage

A. (i) and (iii)
B. (ii) and (iv)
C. (ii), (iv), and (v)
D. (i), (ii), and (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — Spores do not ferment, and sealed cans exclude aerobes, so (i) and (iii) are false. Statements (ii), (iv), and (v) match the lecture directly.

### Q14. Oxidation and lipolytic rancidity result from a reaction between which two components?
A. Reducing sugar and protein
B. Sugar and heat alone
C. Fat and oxygen
D. Starch and water
E. Phenolic compounds and polyphenol oxidase

**Answer: C** — Rancidity is a fat-plus-oxygen reaction. The distractors describe Maillard browning, caramelization, gelatinization, and enzymatic browning respectively.

### Q15. According to the summary slide, causes of food deterioration are grouped into which three categories?
A. Chemical, enzymatic, and microbiological
B. Physical, chemical, or biological
C. Perishable, semi-perishable, and shelf stable
D. Good, bad, and ugly
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — The summary states that food deterioration has physical, chemical, or biological causes. Option A is the wording of the learning objective for types of deterioration factors, not the summary grouping.
