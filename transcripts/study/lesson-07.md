## Flashcards

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the temperature range used for cold storage (refrigeration) of foods? | -2 to 16 degrees C. |
| 2 | What does cold storage / refrigeration do to microorganisms? | It slows down the growth of microorganisms. It does not stop growth and does not kill them. |
| 3 | Is there a single optimal refrigeration temperature for all foods? | No. The optimal temperature varies by food, and some produce is actually damaged at refrigeration temperatures. |
| 4 | Freezing preserves foods by which three mechanisms? | Slowing down microbial growth, stopping microbial growth, and freezing water molecules. |
| 5 | Does freezing kill and eliminate microorganisms? | No. Freezing does NOT kill microorganisms; it freezes water, and the dormant microbes resume growing on thawing. |
| 6 | What happens to water activity when a food is frozen? | It decreases. Liquid water is converted to ice, so free water available to microorganisms drops (freezing does NOT increase water activity). |
| 7 | What is modified atmosphere packaging (MAP)? | The gas mixture inside the package is altered at the time of packing and then left alone. Examples: trays of salami, pre-cut fruit, fresh pasta. |
| 8 | What is controlled atmosphere packaging/storage? | Storage conditions (the gas atmosphere) are actively monitored and maintained over time, as in commercial apple storage rooms. |
| 9 | What is vacuum packaging, and which microorganisms is it especially effective against? | Air is drawn out of the package before sealing. It is especially effective against aerobic spoilage-causing bacteria. |
| 10 | What does vacuum packaging NOT protect against? | Anaerobic organisms. This is why vacuum-packed deli meats still need nitrite and refrigeration. |
| 11 | What is moisture barrier packaging? | Packaging designed to keep water in the food or keep water out of the food. |
| 12 | What is syneresis, and which refrigerated-food changes does it cause? | Syneresis is water being squeezed out of a gel network. In refrigerated foods it causes staling of bread and drip loss in fish. |
| 13 | Which refrigerated-food changes are NOT caused by syneresis? | Colour change in fresh meat (that is pigment chemistry), loss of nutrients, and loss of crispness and flavour in fruits and vegetables. |
| 14 | During freezing, what happens to the volume of water, and why does it matter? | It increases; water expands about 9% as it becomes ice. The expanding ice crystals push against, stretch, and can puncture cell walls. |
| 15 | Rapid (deep) freezing produces what kind of ice crystals, and where? | Many small ice crystals, formed both inside and outside the cells, causing minimal structural damage. |
| 16 | Slow (mild-temperature) freezing produces what kind of ice crystals, and where? | Few large ice crystals, formed mostly outside the cells; they grow large and rupture cell walls, giving mushy thawed product. |
| 17 | What is drip loss? | Loss of cell fluid from a thawed food because large ice crystals ruptured the cell structure during freezing or frozen storage. |
| 18 | Drip loss in thawed strawberries is a result of what? | The formation of large ice crystals due to fluctuating storage temperature. |
| 19 | What is the single most important factor in producing high-quality frozen foods? | Keeping the freezing/storage temperature CONSTANT (not simply keeping it low, and not crystal size alone). |
| 20 | What is recrystallization in frozen foods? | When storage temperature fluctuates, ice partly melts and refreezes elsewhere, so water molecules migrate and join existing crystals, which grow larger. |
| 21 | Ice crystals seen on improperly stored ice cream are a result of what? | Migration of water molecules, caused by fluctuating (not stable) storage temperature. |
| 22 | What is freeze burn? | The dried, discoloured, leathery patches seen on improperly packaged frozen foods such as meat and chicken. |
| 23 | What physical process causes freeze burn? | Sublimation: ice passes directly to vapour and escapes through inadequate packaging, leaving dehydrated tissue behind. |
| 24 | Name the four freezing methods covered in Lesson 7. | Air-blast freezing, indirect contact freezing, immersion freezing, and cryogenic freezing. |
| 25 | What is air-blast freezing, and what specialized equipment does it include? | Cold air is blasted over the food. It includes fluidized-bed freezers, which produce IQF (individually quick frozen) product such as small pieces and chunks. |
| 26 | Strawberries, mango chunks and shrimp are best frozen by which method? | Individually quick frozen (IQF) by fluidized-bed freezers. |
| 27 | What is indirect contact freezing, and what equipment is used? | The food contacts a cold surface but never touches the refrigerant itself. Equipment includes plate freezers and slush freezers. |
| 28 | What is immersion freezing? | The food, usually already packaged, is dunked into a cold liquid; contact is excellent so freezing is very fast. |
| 29 | A turkey (or other large, bagged, irregular food) is frozen by which method? | Immersion freezing. |
| 30 | What is cryogenic freezing? | The extreme, near-instant freezing method, using liquid nitrogen or carbon dioxide. |
| 31 | Which packaging material is NOT suitable for frozen products, and why? | Glass bottles. Water expands on freezing but glass does not, so the glass shatters. |
| 32 | Which packaging materials are standard versus less common for frozen foods? | Standard: cardbox (cardboard) and plastic film. Less common but workable: foil film and Tetra Pak. |
| 33 | How does thawing rate compare with freezing rate, and why? | Thawing is slower than freezing. Water conducts heat better than ice, so the thawed outer layer insulates the still-frozen core. |
| 34 | Why should frozen food be thawed in the refrigerator rather than on the counter? | The warm thawed outer layer is ideal for microbial growth, and freezing never killed those microbes - it only paused them. |
| 35 | Rank the shelf life of pasteurized milk (jug or carton) versus UHT-sterilized Parmalat milk. | UHT-sterilized Parmalat is longest (months, shelf-stable without refrigeration); pasteurized milk plus refrigeration is shortest (days). |

## Cloze facts

- Cold storage (refrigeration) of foods is carried out at {{c1::-2}} to {{c2::16}} degrees C, and it {{c3::slows down but does not stop}} the growth of microorganisms.
- Freezing preserves foods by {{c1::slowing down}} microbial growth, {{c2::stopping}} microbial growth, and {{c3::freezing water molecules}} - it does not kill or eliminate microorganisms.
- Freezing {{c1::lowers}} the water activity of a food because liquid water is converted to {{c2::ice}}, which is {{c3::not available}} to microorganisms.
- During freezing the volume of water {{c1::increases}}, expanding by about {{c2::9}}%, which is exactly why {{c3::glass bottles}} are not suitable packaging for frozen products.
- {{c1::Rapid (deep)}} freezing produces {{c2::many small}} ice crystals both inside and outside the cells, whereas {{c3::slow (mild-temperature)}} freezing produces few large crystals mostly outside the cells that rupture cell walls.
- The most important factor in producing high quality frozen foods is keeping the freezing temperature {{c1::constant}}, because fluctuation causes {{c2::recrystallization}} driven by {{c3::migration of water molecules}}.
- {{c1::Freeze burn}} is the dried, discoloured patch on improperly packaged frozen meat and chicken, caused by {{c2::sublimation}} of ice escaping through {{c3::inadequate packaging}}.
- The four freezing methods are {{c1::air-blast}} freezing (fluidized-bed freezers, IQF), {{c2::indirect contact}} freezing (plate and slush freezers), immersion freezing, and {{c3::cryogenic}} freezing (liquid nitrogen or carbon dioxide).
- {{c1::Strawberries, mango chunks and shrimp}} are best frozen individually quick frozen (IQF) in {{c2::fluidized-bed}} freezers, whereas a {{c3::turkey}} is frozen by immersion freezing.
- Vacuum packaging is especially effective against {{c1::aerobic}} spoilage-causing bacteria but not against {{c2::anaerobic}} organisms, while {{c3::modified atmosphere}} packaging alters the gas mix once at packing time and controlled atmosphere packaging actively maintains it.
- Syneresis in refrigerated foods causes {{c1::staling of bread}} and {{c2::drip loss in fish}}, but it does not cause {{c3::colour change in fresh meat}} or loss of nutrients.
- Drip loss in thawed strawberries results from {{c1::large}} ice crystals formed because of {{c2::fluctuating storage temperature}}, and thawing is {{c3::slower}} than freezing because water conducts heat better than ice.

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 07: Low Temperature Preservation — Practice MCQs

### Q1. Cold storage (refrigeration) of foods is carried out over which temperature range?
A. -18 to -2 °C
B. -2 to 16 °C
C. 0 to 4 °C
D. 4 to 20 °C
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — The lecture defines cold storage as -2 to 16 °C. Note that a single "fridge temperature" is not specified because the optimal temperature varies by food.

---

### Q2. Freezing preserves foods by...
(i) Slowing down microbial growth
(ii) Stopping microbial growth
(iii) Killing and eliminating all microorganisms
(iv) Freezing water molecules
(v) Increasing water activity

A. (i) and (ii)
B. (i), (ii), (iii)
C. (i), (ii), (iv)
D. (i), (ii), (v)
E. All of the factors

**Answer: C** — Freezing slows and stops microbial growth and freezes water molecules. It does NOT kill microorganisms, and it *lowers* rather than increases water activity.

---

### Q3. Syneresis leads to the following chemical change(s) in refrigerated foods.
(i) Staling of bread
(ii) Loss of crispness and flavour in fruits and vegetables
(iii) Colour change in fresh meat
(iv) Loss of nutrients
(v) Drip loss in fish

A. (i)
B. (ii)
C. (iii)
D. (v)
E. (i) and (v)

**Answer: E** — Syneresis is water squeezed out of a gel network, which shows up as bread staling and drip loss in fish. Meat colour change is pigment chemistry, not syneresis.

---

### Q4. Vacuum packaging is especially effective against the growth of...
A. Aerobic spoilage causing bacteria
B. Anaerobic spoilage causing bacteria
C. Psychrotrophic spoilage causing bacteria
D. All of the above
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — Removing air removes oxygen, which starves oxygen-dependent spoilage organisms. Anaerobes are unaffected (and even favoured), which is why vacuum-packed deli meats still rely on nitrite and refrigeration.

---

### Q5. The most important factor in producing high quality frozen foods is...
A. Forming small ice crystals
B. Forming large ice crystals
C. Keeping the freezing temperature low
D. Keeping the freezing temperature constant
E. Rapid freezing and thawing

**Answer: D** — Small crystals and low temperature are both desirable, but a constant temperature is the single most important factor. A steady -18 °C outperforms a fluctuating -25 °C.

---

### Q6. Ice crystals seen on improperly stored ice cream are a result of...
A. Storage temperature not low enough
B. Inappropriate freezing method
C. Stable storage temperature
D. Migration of water molecules
E. Sublimation through the packaging

**Answer: D** — Temperature fluctuation melts and refreezes ice so water molecules migrate and join existing crystals (recrystallization). Sublimation through packaging is freeze burn, a different defect.

---

### Q7. Drip loss in thawed strawberries is a result of...
A. The formation of small ice crystals at ultra-low temperature freezing
B. The formation of small ice crystals both inside and outside of the cell structure
C. The formation of large ice crystals due to fluctuating storage temperature
D. The formation of large ice crystals during rapid freezing process
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — Large crystals rupture cell walls, so fluid pours out on thawing. Option D is self-contradictory: rapid freezing yields many *small* crystals, not large ones.

---

### Q8. Name this undesirable phenomenon: dried, discoloured, leathery patches on improperly packaged frozen meat and chicken.
A. Freeze burn
B. Freeze cooking
C. Freeze crystallization
D. Freeze dehydration
E. Freeze dry

**Answer: A** — Freeze burn is caused by sublimation, where ice passes directly to vapour and escapes through inadequate packaging, leaving dehydrated tissue behind.

---

### Q9. Strawberries, mango chunks and shrimps are best frozen by this method.
A. Individually quick frozen by fluidized-bed freezers
B. Individually quick frozen by cryogenic freezing
C. Slush freezer
D. Immersion freezing
E. Plate freezing

**Answer: A** — Small pieces and chunks are IQF'd in fluidized-bed (air-blast) freezers, which is why frozen berries pour out loose instead of as one solid block.

---

### Q10. A turkey (or other large, bagged, irregularly shaped food) is frozen by which method?
A. Individually quick frozen by fluidized-bed freezers
B. Individually quick frozen by cryogenic freezing
C. Slush freezer
D. Immersion freezing
E. Plate freezing

**Answer: D** — The already-packaged bird is dunked into a cold liquid, giving fast freezing with excellent contact over an irregular shape. Fluidized beds suit small free-flowing pieces, not whole birds.

---

### Q11. Which package material below is NOT suitable for frozen products?
A. Cardbox
B. Foil film
C. Glass bottle
D. Plastic film
E. Tetra pak

**Answer: C** — Water expands as it freezes but glass does not, so the bottle shatters. Cardbox and plastic film are standard; foil film and Tetra Pak are less common but workable.

---

### Q12. During freezing, the volume of water:
A. Increases
B. Decreases
C. Stabilizes
D. All of the above
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — Water expands (about 9%) as it turns to ice. This expansion is what lets growing ice crystals stretch and puncture cell walls, and what shatters glass containers.

---

### Q13. Which of the following statements about thawing frozen foods is/are correct?
(i) Thawing is slower than freezing
(ii) Water conducts heat better than ice, so the thawed outer layer insulates the frozen core
(iii) The warm outer layer supports microbial growth during counter-top thawing
(iv) Microorganisms that were dormant in the frozen food resume growth on thawing
(v) Thawing destroys the microorganisms that survived freezing

A. (i), (ii)
B. (iii), (iv), (v)
C. (i), (ii), (iii), (iv)
D. (ii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — Statements (i) through (iv) are all correct; only (v) is false, since neither freezing nor thawing kills microorganisms — they were merely paused.

---

### Q14. A tray of sliced salami, a package of fresh pasta and a tray of pre-cut fruit all have the internal gas mixture altered at the time of packing and are then sealed and left alone. This is:
A. Controlled atmosphere packaging
B. Modified atmosphere packaging
C. Moisture barrier packaging
D. Vacuum packaging
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — In MAP the atmosphere is modified once at packing. Controlled atmosphere packaging (as in apple storage rooms) actively maintains the conditions over time; vacuum packaging removes air rather than replacing it.
