## Cloze facts

- Food science studies the {{c1::production}}, {{c2::processing, preparing}}, {{c3::evaluating, and use}} of food.
- The food industry's job from farm gate to plate is to keep food {{c1::safe}}, {{c2::nutritious}}, and {{c3::tasty}}.
- The five sub-fields of food science are {{c1::food chemistry}}, {{c2::food analysis}}, {{c3::food microbiology}}, {{c4::food processing}}, and {{c5::food engineering}}.
- Matching the sub-fields: structure, composition and properties of foods = {{c1::food chemistry}}; roles of micro-organisms in food borne illness and fermentation = {{c2::food microbiology}}; thermodynamics and energy transfer during processing = {{c3::food engineering}}.
- A statue of {{c1::Francois Nicolas Appert}} is placed outside the headquarters of the {{c2::Institute of Food Technologists (IFT)}} to recognize his invention of {{c3::canning}}.
- IFT-approved Food Science programs must cover {{c1::55}} essential learning outcomes (ELOs), and IFT also publishes an annual {{c2::Top Ten Food Trends}} list.
- The major cereal-grain-producing provinces of Canada are {{c1::Alberta}}, {{c2::Saskatchewan}}, and {{c3::Manitoba}}.
- Apples in BC are typically harvested in {{c1::October}}, and normal air contains about {{c2::21}}% oxygen.
- Controlled atmosphere storage controls {{c1::temperature}}, {{c2::humidity}}, {{c3::oxygen}}, and {{c4::carbon dioxide}} - the answer is "all of the above".
- In apple cider production, {{c1::yeast}} is added to apple juice and yields {{c2::ethanol}}; the confusable pairing, {{c3::bacterial culture}} plus acetic acid, gives vinegar instead.

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 01 Practice MCQs
### Food Science and the Canadian Food System

*All content drawn from the Lesson 01 lecture deck and podcast transcript.*

### Q1. Food science studies the __________ of food.

A. production, processing, preparing, evaluating, and use
B. production, marketing, retailing, and consumption
C. cultivation, harvesting, and cooking only
D. chemistry, microbiology, and engineering only
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — This is the course's exact definition of food science. Option D lists sub-fields of the discipline, not the activities studied, and is the most tempting near-miss.

---

### Q2. A statue of ___(i)___ is placed outside of the headquarter of the Institute of Food Technologists (ift.org) to recognize his invention of ___(ii)___.

A. (i) an ancient Egyptian, (ii) canning
B. (i) an ancient Egyptian, (ii) winemaking
C. (i) an ancient Egyptian, (ii) fermentation
D. (i) Francois Nicolas Appert, (ii) canning
E. (i) Francois Nicolas Appert, (ii) winemaking

**Answer: D** — Appert invented canning in Napoleon-era France, before anyone knew microorganisms existed. The "ancient Egyptian" and "winemaking" options are the standard lures.

---

### Q3. Which of the following are among the five sub-fields of food science named in FNH 200?

(i) Food Chemistry
(ii) Food Analysis
(iii) Food Toxicology
(iv) Food Engineering
(v) Food Marketing

A. (i), (ii)
B. (i), (ii), (iii)
C. (i), (ii), (iv)
D. (i), (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — The five sub-fields are Food Chemistry, Food Analysis, Food Microbiology, Food Processing, and Food Engineering. "Food Toxicology" is a trap: toxicants appear in Lesson 12 but toxicology is not one of the five named sub-fields.

---

### Q4. Which Canadian provinces are the major producers of cereal grains?

(i) BC
(ii) Alberta
(iii) Saskatchewan
(iv) Manitoba
(v) Ontario

A. (ii) only
B. (iii) only
C. (iv) only
D. (iii) and (iv)
E. (ii), (iii), and (iv)

**Answer: E** — All three prairie provinces are major cereal grain producers. "Saskatchewan only" is the classic wrong answer; BC and Ontario are not the grain giants.

---

### Q5. What is being controlled in controlled atmosphere storage?

A. Temperature
B. Humidity
C. Oxygen
D. Carbon dioxide
E. All of the above

**Answer: E** — Controlled atmosphere storage regulates temperature, humidity, oxygen, and carbon dioxide together. Each single-factor option is true but incomplete.

---

### Q6. How much oxygen is in the atmosphere?

A. 0.3%
B. 3.0%
C. 21%
D. 78%
E. 94%

**Answer: C** — Normal air is about 21% oxygen. 78% is the classic confusable (that figure belongs to the other main atmospheric gas, not oxygen).

---

### Q7. In the production of apple cider, ___(i)___ is added to apple juice and yields the production of ___(ii)___.

A. (i) yeast; (ii) ethanol
B. (i) yeast; (ii) acetic acid
C. (i) bacterial culture; (ii) ethanol
D. (i) bacterial culture; (ii) acetic acid
E. (i) pectin; (ii) ethanol

**Answer: A** — Yeast ferments apple juice to ethanol. The bacteria/acetic acid combination (option D) produces vinegar, not cider.

---

### Q8. What is an example of a value-added product made from apple juice waste?

A. Cider
B. Syrup
C. Fruit leather
D. Pectin
E. Animal feed

**Answer: D** — The pressed-out pulp left over from juicing is used to extract pectin, the gelling agent that sets jams and jellies. Cider is made from the juice itself, not the waste.

---

### Q9. "Thermodynamics, energy transfer during the processing of foods" describes which sub-field of food science?

A. Food Chemistry
B. Food Analysis
C. Food Processing
D. Food Engineering
E. Food Microbiology

**Answer: D** — Food Engineering, which FNH 200 covers only very briefly. Food Processing (option C) is the near-miss: it covers manufacturing, packaging, and handling of foods.

---

### Q10. Which of the following statements about the Institute of Food Technologists (IFT) is correct?

A. IFT establishes 15 essential learning outcomes for approved Food Science degree programs.
B. IFT is the Canadian federal agency responsible for enforcing food standards and regulations.
C. IFT publishes an annual Top Ten Food Trends list and approves undergraduate Food Science programs.
D. IFT's headquarters displays a statue honouring the invention of winemaking.
E. None of the above

**Answer: C** — IFT sets undergraduate education standards for Food Science degrees and publishes the Top Ten Food Trends. Option A gets the number wrong (it is 55 ELOs, not 15); IFT is an international professional organization, not a Canadian regulator.

---

### Q11. Typical harvesting month for apples in BC is...

A. January
B. April
C. July
D. December
E. None of the above

**Answer: E** — BC apples are typically harvested in October, which is not listed. One month of harvest supplies twelve months of apples in stores via controlled atmosphere storage.

---

### Q12. Why is the oxygen level lowered around apples in controlled atmosphere storage?

A. To prevent bacterial fermentation that would convert sugars to ethanol
B. To slow the apple's respiration and ripening, because a harvested apple is still alive
C. To increase the pectin content so the fruit firms up during storage
D. To raise the relative humidity inside the storage room
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — The harvested apple continues to respire, ripen, and age; dropping oxygen, chilling the room, and raising CO2 slows its metabolism to a crawl. Ethanol production (A) belongs to cider making, and pectin (C) to juice waste.

### Q13. Which of the following are stated in the Lesson 01 summary?

(i) Canada offers a vast and diverse food system.
(ii) The Canadian food industry has a strong impact on Canada's economy.
(iii) Consumers and consumer demands have a strong influence on food consumption trends.
(iv) Technological advancements have led to many food science discoveries.
(v) Cereal grain production is Canada's single largest food export.

A. (i), (ii)
B. (i), (ii), (iii)
C. (i), (ii), (iii), (iv)
D. (ii), (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — Statements (i) through (iv) are the four content bullets of the Lesson 01 summary. Statement (v) is never made; the deck only identifies which provinces produce cereal grains.

### Q14. Among the apple products compared in the Lesson 01 group activity, which requires the LEAST processing before reaching the consumer?

A. Apple sauce
B. Apple juice
C. Apple cider
D. A fresh, locally grown apple
E. An apple imported from New Zealand

**Answer: D** — A fresh local apple is minimally processed (washed, waxed, stored). Sauce is chopped/cooked/packed, juice is pressed/filtered/pasteurized, cider adds a fermentation step, and the imported apple adds a full cold chain of storage, shipping, and logistics.
