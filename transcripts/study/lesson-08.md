# Lesson 08: Dehydration — Study Deck

35 flashcards · 12 cloze · 15 MCQs

## Flashcards

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the principle of dehydration? | Removal of free water AND lowering of water activity. It is NOT elimination of pathogenic or spoilage-causing microorganisms. |
| 2 | Does dehydration kill or eliminate microorganisms in food? | No. Drying only lowers water activity so microbes cannot grow - like freezing, which immobilizes free water as ice instead of removing it. Pathogens and spoilage organisms survive and grow again once the food is rehydrated and held warm. |
| 3 | During dehydration, what happens to water in the food? (exam wording) | It is removed from the surface of the food first, AND it is often trapped in the centre. Both statements are true. |
| 4 | What is case hardening? | When the surface dries too fast it forms a tough, sealed shell while the inside is still wet, so trapped moisture cannot escape. The result is a leathery crust around a damp core - unstable and prone to spoiling from within. |
| 5 | How is case hardening prevented? | Dry gently: lower temperature and longer drying time, so the moisture gradient stays manageable. |
| 6 | What is the white powder seen on the surface of dried pineapple or mango? | Sugar. |
| 7 | How does the white sugar powder get onto the surface of dried pineapple? | It was left behind on the surface as water was removed. It was not added; water evaporates but dissolved sugar cannot, so it crystallizes out on the surface. |
| 8 | Name the seven dehydration methods covered in Lesson 08. | Sun drying, tray (hot air) drying, spray drying, vacuum microwave drying, drum drying, deep fat frying, extrusion drying. |
| 9 | What is the most common form of dehydration worldwide? | Sun drying. |
| 10 | Why is sun drying so widely used, and what does it require? | It is the oldest method, cheap and needs no equipment; it does require good weather. |
| 11 | Give signature foods dried by sun drying. | Raisins, dried shiitake mushrooms, dried mango. |
| 12 | Describe tray (hot air) drying. | Food is placed on trays and hot air is blown over it. Simple and industrial, but the heat damages colour and flavour. |
| 13 | Describe how spray drying works. | A liquid is atomized into a fine mist inside a hot chamber; the tiny droplets dry almost instantly and fall out as a powder. |
| 14 | Skim milk powder, instant coffee, and other liquid foods are often dried by which method? | Spray drying. Exam shortcut: if the question says liquid turned into a powder, the answer is spray drying. |
| 15 | Describe how drum drying works. | A slurry is spread as a thin film on a hot rotating drum heated by 120 degrees C steam inside; the thin dry layer is scraped off and falls into a solids collector. |
| 16 | What is a doctor blade and where is it used? | The blade in a drum drier that scrapes the thin dried sheet off the surface of the rotating drum. |
| 17 | What is the signature product of drum drying? | Instant mashed potato (potato slurry dried to flakes). |
| 18 | Which dehydration technique was developed at UBC? | Vacuum microwave drying. |
| 19 | How does vacuum microwave drying work and why is it good? | Microwaves heat the food from within while a vacuum lets water boil off at low temperature. It is fast and gentle, so colour and nutrients are retained. |
| 20 | In deep fat frying, which functional property of fat (from Lesson 02) makes it work as a drying medium? | Fat withstands high temperature. The hot oil flashes the water out of the food. |
| 21 | What is the important property of deep fat-fried instant noodles? | Rapid rehydration rate due to the voids left as water evaporated. Escaping steam leaves a sponge of tiny channels that boiling water floods back into. |
| 22 | Describe extrusion drying. | Dough is forced under heat and pressure through a die; when it exits into normal pressure the trapped steam flashes off and the product puffs. |
| 23 | Give signature foods made by extrusion drying. | Cheerios, cheese puffs, shrimp crackers, veggie straws. |
| 24 | What are the two steps of freeze drying? | (1) Freeze the food solid. (2) Dry it under vacuum (low pressure, low temperature) so the ice sublimes. |
| 25 | What is sublimation? | The change of a solid directly to vapour, skipping the liquid phase. In freeze drying, ice goes straight to water vapour. |
| 26 | Sublimation appears twice in the course - as a defect and as a process. Where? | As freeze burn (freezer burn) during frozen storage, and as the deliberate, controlled drying step in freeze drying. |
| 27 | Why does freeze-dried food keep its original size and shape? | Water leaves as vapour from wherever the ice crystals were, and each crystal leaves behind a void, so the structure never collapses. |
| 28 | List the quality advantages of freeze-dried food. | Keeps its original size and shape (an air-dried strawberry shrivels to about half size), keeps bright colour, rehydrates faster and better, and has excellent flavour and nutrient retention. |
| 29 | What allows freeze-dried strawberries to stay bright pink? | Low temperature drying under vacuum. No heat means no browning and no pigment damage - not added colourants. |
| 30 | Air-dried vs freeze-dried strawberry: which rehydrates better and faster, and why? | The freeze-dried one, because the voids left by sublimed ice crystals are open channels for water to rush back in. |
| 31 | What are the disadvantages of freeze drying, and where is it used anyway? | It is slow and expensive (freezing plus vacuum chambers). Used where quality justifies cost: premium instant coffee, astronaut food, camping meals. |
| 32 | How do spray-dried and freeze-dried instant coffee differ in appearance? | Spray dried is fine, dark and powdery; freeze dried is larger, lighter-coloured, glassy granules. |
| 33 | What type of packaging material is best for dried foods, and why? | Tin card (foil), because it blocks light and prevents oxidation. It is also a moisture barrier - hence the metallized lining of chip bags. |
| 34 | Why do dried foods need protection from oxygen and light? | They are high-surface-area products and are prone to oxidative rancidity; the package must also keep water out after all that water was removed. |
| 35 | Why are plastic, cardboard, and glass not the best packaging for dried foods? | Plastic alone is a decent water barrier but lets light through; cardboard prevents crushing but not moisture; glass appeals to consumers but does nothing useful here. |

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 08: Dehydration — Practice MCQs

### Q1. The principle of dehydration is...
(i) Elimination of pathogenic microorganisms
(ii) Elimination of spoilage-causing microorganisms
(iii) Removal of free water
(iv) Lowering of water activity

A. (i), (ii)
B. (iii), (iv)
C. (i), (iii), (iv)
D. (ii), (iii), (iv)
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — Dehydration removes free water and lowers water activity so microbes cannot grow; it does not eliminate pathogens or spoilage organisms, which survive and can grow again after rehydration.

### Q2. During dehydration, water in foods:
A. Is being removed from the surface of the foods first
B. Is removed from the centre first
C. Is often trapped in the centre
D. (A) and (C)
E. (A), (B), and (C)

**Answer: D** — The drying front moves inward, so surface water leaves first while interior water must diffuse outward and is often trapped in the centre.

### Q3. A dried prune has a tough, leathery outer shell but a damp interior that later spoils. What happened, and how should it have been prevented?
A. Sugar bloom; the fruit should have been rinsed before drying
B. Case hardening; the fruit should have been dried more slowly at a lower temperature
C. Case hardening; the fruit should have been dried faster at a higher temperature
D. Freeze burn; the fruit should have been packaged in foil
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — Case hardening occurs when the surface dries too quickly and seals, trapping moisture inside; gentler drying (lower temperature, longer time) keeps the moisture gradient manageable.

### Q4. What is the white powder seen on the surface of dried pineapple, and how does it get there?
A. Sugar; it was added to help draw the water out of the pineapple
B. Sugar; it was left on the surface as water was removed
C. Salt; to draw water out
D. Ascorbic acid; as a preservative against browning
E. Calcium carbonate; added as an anticaking agent

**Answer: B** — Water evaporates from the surface but the dissolved sugar cannot, so it is left behind and crystallizes on the surface. Nothing was added.

### Q5. Skim milk powder, instant coffee, and other liquid "foods" are often dried by:
A. Sun drying
B. Spray drying
C. Tray (hot air) drying
D. Drum drying
E. Extrusion drying

**Answer: B** — Spray drying atomizes a liquid into a fine mist in a hot chamber; the tiny droplets dry almost instantly and fall out as a powder. Drum drying is for slurries such as potato, not free-flowing liquid to powder.

### Q6. A food processor needs to convert a thick potato slurry into flakes for instant mashed potato. Which method, and which piece of equipment removes the dried product?
A. Spray drying; a cyclone separator
B. Drum drying; a doctor blade
C. Drum drying; an atomizing nozzle
D. Extrusion drying; a die
E. Vacuum microwave drying; a solids collector

**Answer: B** — Drum drying spreads the slurry as a thin film on a drum heated by 120 °C steam, and a doctor blade scrapes the thin dried layer off into the solids collector.

### Q7. Which of the following statements about drying methods is/are TRUE?
(i) Sun drying is the most common form of dehydration worldwide
(ii) Vacuum microwave drying was developed at UBC
(iii) Spray drying is the most common form of dehydration worldwide
(iv) Tray (hot air) drying damages colour and flavour
(v) Extrusion drying produces a puffed product

A. (i), (ii)
B. (iii), (iv), (v)
C. (i), (ii), (iv), (v)
D. (ii), (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: C** — Sun drying (not spray drying) is the most common worldwide, so (iii) is false; all the other statements are correct.

### Q8. Which technique below was developed at UBC?
A. Sun drying
B. Spray drying
C. Tray (hot air) drying
D. Vacuum microwave drying
E. Drum drying

**Answer: D** — Vacuum microwave drying is the UBC-developed method: microwaves heat the food from within while the vacuum lets water boil off at low temperature, so it is both fast and gentle on colour and nutrients.

### Q9. An important property of deep fat-fried instant noodles is...
A. Rapid rehydration rate as the noodles were dried rapidly
B. Rapid rehydration rate due to voids left as water evaporated
C. Puff structure was created due to vacuum
D. Polysaccharide used in the product has a very high water absorption (rehydration) rate
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — Deep fat frying works because fat withstands high temperature (Lesson 02); escaping steam leaves a sponge of tiny voids and channels, so boiling water floods back in within minutes. The speed of drying itself, a vacuum, and the polysaccharide are not the reason.

### Q10. Cheerios, cheese puffs, shrimp crackers, and veggie straws are produced by which method, and what makes them puff?
A. Spray drying; droplets expand in the hot chamber
B. Drum drying; steam blisters form under the doctor blade
C. Extrusion drying; steam flashes off as the dough exits the die into normal pressure
D. Deep fat frying; oil vapour expands inside the dough
E. Freeze drying; ice crystals expand during sublimation

**Answer: C** — In extrusion drying, dough is forced under heat and pressure through a die; on exit the trapped steam flashes and the product puffs.

### Q11. Which statements about freeze drying is/are correct?
(i) The food is frozen first, then dried under vacuum
(ii) The ice melts and the liquid water is then evaporated
(iii) The ice sublimes, going directly from solid to vapour
(iv) Each ice crystal leaves behind a void in the food
(v) It is a cheap and rapid method

A. (i), (iii), (iv)
B. (i), (ii), (iv)
C. (iii), (iv), (v)
D. (i), (iii), (iv), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — Freeze drying is freeze then dry under vacuum so the ice sublimes without ever melting, leaving a void where each crystal was; it is slow and expensive, not cheap and rapid.

### Q12. What allows the production of these bright pink freeze-dried strawberries?
A. Addition of colourants
B. Sun drying at low temperature
C. Low temperature drying under vacuum
D. Microwave drying with direct heat entering the centre
E. None of the above

**Answer: C** — Freeze drying dries at low temperature under vacuum, so there is no heat-induced browning or pigment damage and the colour stays vivid.

### Q13. Two dried strawberry samples: sample A is dark, shrivelled and about half its original size; sample B is full-sized, bright pink and shatters when bitten. Which is true?
A. A was freeze dried and rehydrates better
B. B was air dried and rehydrates better
C. A was air dried, and B rehydrates better because of the voids left by sublimed ice crystals
D. B was freeze dried, but A rehydrates better because it is denser
E. Both rehydrate equally well because both have the same final water activity

**Answer: C** — The shrivelled dark sample is air dried; the freeze-dried sample keeps its size, shape and colour and rehydrates faster and better because the sublimed ice crystals leave open channels.

### Q14. Sublimation appears in this course both as a storage defect and as a deliberate processing step. Which pairing is correct?
A. Freeze burn is sublimation of ice in frozen storage; freeze drying uses controlled sublimation under vacuum
B. Freeze burn is oxidation of fat; freeze drying uses evaporation of liquid water
C. Case hardening is sublimation at the surface; freeze drying uses melting then evaporation
D. Freeze burn is sublimation; freeze drying avoids sublimation by melting the ice first
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — Freeze burn and freeze drying are the same physics — ice going straight to vapour — except that in freeze drying it is wanted and controlled under vacuum.

### Q15. What type of packaging material is best for dried foods?
A. Plastic as it is a good water barrier
B. Cardboard as it prevents physical damage
C. Tin card (foil) as it blocks light and prevents oxidation
D. Glass jar as it appeals well to consumers
E. All of the above are equally suitable

**Answer: C** — Dried foods are high-surface-area and prone to oxidative rancidity, so the package must exclude light, oxygen and moisture; foil does all three, which is why chip bags are metallized inside.
