| 1 | What is a colloidal dispersion, and what are its two phases? | A system in which the particles of one substance are distributed (dispersed) in another substance without dissolving. Dispersed phase = the substance dispersed within another; continuous phase = the substance that extends throughout the system and surrounds it (the exterior/outside). |
| 2 | Table 2.1: a SOLID dispersed in a LIQUID is called what, and what does it do in food? | A sol - e.g. starches, proteins and some plant polysaccharides in water. A sol helps hold small pieces of food suspended in the liquid (e.g. salad dressing). |
| 3 | Table 2.1: a LIQUID dispersed in a LIQUID is called what? Examples? | An emulsion. Examples: milk and mayonnaise. |
| 4 | Table 2.1: a LIQUID dispersed in a SOLID is called what? Examples? | A solid emulsion. Examples: butter and margarine (both are water-in-oil). |
| 5 | Table 2.1: which dispersion is a liquid held in a solid network, and what are its examples? | A gel. Examples: starch, pectin or gelatin gels; also tofu and sausages. |
| 6 | Table 2.1: a GAS dispersed in a LIQUID is called what? Examples? | A foam. Examples: beaten egg white and whipped cake frostings. |
| 7 | Table 2.1: a GAS dispersed in a SOLID is called what? Examples? | A solid foam. Examples: meringue, ice cream and bread. Ice cream must contain at least 36% solids - the rest is largely whipped-in gas. |
| 8 | Which is the MOST accurate description of milk, and how does margarine differ? | Milk is an oil-in-water emulsion (fat droplets dispersed in a continuous water phase). Margarine and butter are the reverse - water-in-oil solid emulsions. |
| 9 | What type of food colloidal dispersion is angel cake or marshmallow? | Solid foam (gas dispersed in a solid). |
| 10 | Why does the fat not float to the top (cream) in commercially available milk? | Each fat droplet is coated by natural milk protein acting as an emulsifier, and the droplets have been made small, so they stay suspended instead of creaming. |
| 11 | Distinguish an emulsifier from a stabilizer, and define creaming. | An emulsifier sits at the oil-water interface and coats the droplets; a stabilizer thickens the continuous phase so droplets cannot rise. Creaming is droplets floating to the top - salad dressings add a stabilizer to prevent it. |
| 12 | What are phospholipids, why are they great emulsifiers, and name the common food example. | Phospholipids are a special fat that is partly water-liking (hydrophilic) and partly oil/fat-liking (hydrophobic), so they sit at the oil-water interface. Lecithin, found in egg yolk and soybean, is the commonly used one. |
| 13 | Name the three carbohydrate classes, the three disaccharides and the enzyme that digests each. | Monosaccharides (single unit): glucose, fructose, galactose. Disaccharides: sucrose = glu-fru (invertase), lactose = glu-gal (lactase), maltose = glu-glu (maltase). Polysaccharides: very long chains of glucose, e.g. starch. |
| 14 | Table 2.2: give the sweetness index of sucrose, fructose, glucose, galactose, maltose and lactose. | Sucrose 100 (the benchmark), fructose (levulose) 140, glucose (dextrose) 70-80, galactose 60, maltose 20, lactose 10-20. |
| 15 | Table 2.2: give the sweetness index of invert sugar, corn syrup, sorbitol, xylitol and the high fructose corn syrups. | Invert sugar 100-130, corn syrup (glucose/maltose mixture) 50, sorbitol 50, xylitol 100. HFCS: 42% fructose = 100, 55% fructose = 100+, 90% fructose = 120-160. |
| 16 | Why was the deck's ranking question that included generic 'corn syrup' unanswerable? | It is a tricky question - there is not enough information to determine the ranking, because corn syrup is a variable mixture of glucose and maltose. |
| 17 | Outline the reaction pathway used to make high fructose corn syrup. | Starch (amylose, not sweet) -> amylase or acid -> maltose (slightly sweet) -> maltase -> glucose (sweet) -> glucose isomerase -> fructose (very sweet). |
| 18 | Why is lactose-free milk sweeter than regular milk, and what is invert sugar? | Added lactase splits lactose (index 10-20) into glucose (70-80) and galactose (60), which are sweeter. Invert sugar is sucrose split by invertase or acid into glucose + fructose, giving a sweeter mixture (100-130) than sucrose (100). |
| 19 | In addition to acting as a sweetening agent, what is the primary role of sugar in jam? | Preservative. Sugar binds water, converting free water to bound water so microbes cannot use it. |
| 20 | Contrast the Maillard reaction with caramelization, and name a browning-question item that is neither. | Maillard = sugar AND protein (a reducing sugar plus an amino compound); e.g. bread, toast, coffee, roasted foods. Caramelization = sugar ONLY; e.g. caramel, toffee. Sugar cane is NOT a Maillard product. |
| 21 | What is gelatinization? | What cooking does to starch: starch granules absorb water and swell (e.g. cooking rice or pasta). |
| 22 | What is retrogradation? | The starch recrystallizing so that cooked rice, pasta or bread turns hard again after cooking (e.g. leftovers in the fridge). |
| 23 | What is syneresis, and what are dextrins? | Syneresis is water being squeezed out of a gel (a gel weeping liquid). Dextrins are smaller starch fragments - a term revisited in Lesson 4. |
| 24 | Give the source and food use of pectins, agar and alginates. | Pectins: structural polymers in plants such as apple, the cement between plant cells; gelling agent in jams and jellies, viscosity in tomato paste and ketchup, mouth feel in juice. Agar: extracted from seaweed (kelp), used as a thickener. Alginates: from certain seaweeds, gelling agents that keep solids and liquids suspended in fruit juices. |
| 25 | Give the source and food use of gum arabic, carrageenan and xanthan gum. | Gum arabic (gum acacia): plant exudate from the bark of the acacia tree, thickener and stabilizer. Carrageenan: from certain seaweeds (red algae), suspending agent that keeps cocoa particles suspended in chocolate milk. Xanthan gum: produced by bacteria, first isolated from rotting cabbage, thickener in salad dressings. |
| 26 | List the seven functional roles of fat in food science, and say which one explains deep-fat frying french fries. | Aerate, carry aroma, emulsify, lubricate, soften, tenderize, withstand high temperature. French fries cook quickly because oil can withstand high temperature. |
| 27 | What are the functional roles of proteins in foods, and which milk component makes latte foam? | Proteins act as enzymes and form foams and gels (bread crumb, whipped egg white, cooked egg, sausages, tofu). Milk protein - not milk fat, lactose or other carbohydrate - is responsible for the foam on a latte. |
| 28 | What is the unwelcome role of proteins in foods, and who identifies Canada's list? | Food allergens are proteins. Health Canada and the CFIA identify Canada's priority food allergens: eggs, milk, mustard, peanuts, crustaceans and molluscs, fish, sesame, soy, sulphites, tree nuts and wheat. |
| 29 | What is the most important component in food, and what are the two types of it? | Water. Bound water is bound to food components (salt, sugar, fat, protein) or to the container; free water is available for microbial growth, chemical reactions and enzymatic reactions. |
| 30 | What is water activity, and why can foods not be ranked by moisture content alone? | Water activity (aw) measures the free/available water on a 0 to 1 scale (pure water = 1.0), defined by vapour pressure at equilibrium. Moisture content is not water activity - given apple sauce 95%, fresh egg 75%, fresh meat 72% and apple jelly 35% moisture, you need more data, because the jelly's sugar binds much of its water. |
| 31 | Give the minimum water activity for growth of most bacteria, yeasts and molds, plus the two specialist groups. | Most bacteria aw 0.91, most yeasts aw 0.88, most molds aw 0.80. Osmophilic yeasts go down to aw 0.62 and xerophilic molds to aw 0.65. |
| 32 | List the minor constituents of foods. | Acids, pigments, aroma constituents, vitamins and minerals - present in small amounts but important to food quality and safety. |
| 33 | Name the major acid in apple, and match citric, tartaric and lactic acid to their sources. | Malic acid is the major acid in apple. Citric acid = citrus, tartaric acid = grapes, lactic acid = fermented dairy. |
| 34 | In terms of food safety, which pH is the most critical, and what does it separate? | pH 4.6. A food with pH below 4.6 is low pH / highly acidic (high-acid); a food above pH 4.6 is low-acid. |
| 35 | Which vitamins have additional functions as food additives, and what does calcium sulphate do in tofu? | Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) is a bleaching agent (hastens oxidation and aging as in flour whitening) and a preservative antioxidant that slows rancidity and browning; tocopherols (vitamin E) are preservative antioxidants. Calcium sulphate is the coagulant that sets soy milk into tofu (a gel). |

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 02 Practice MCQs
### Chemical and Physical Properties of Foods

*All content drawn from the Lesson 02 lecture deck and podcast transcript.*

---

### Q1. Which one of the following is the most accurate description of milk?

A. Milk is a liquid.
B. Milk is an aqueous solution.
C. Milk is an emulsion.
D. Milk is an oil-in-water emulsion.
E. Milk is a water-in-oil emulsion.

**Answer: D** — "Milk is an emulsion" (C) is true but not the *most* accurate; the fat droplets are the dispersed phase inside a continuous water phase. Water-in-oil (E) describes butter and margarine.

---

### Q2. What type of food colloidal dispersion is angel cake or marshmallow?

A. Emulsion
B. Solid emulsion
C. Liquid foam
D. Solid foam
E. None of the above

**Answer: D** — Both are gas dispersed in a solid continuous phase, the same class as meringue, bread and ice cream. A liquid foam (C) is gas in a liquid, e.g. beaten egg white before baking.

---

### Q3. Consider the following food/dispersion pairs:

(i) mayonnaise — emulsion
(ii) butter — solid emulsion
(iii) ice cream — sol
(iv) beaten egg white — foam
(v) gelatin dessert — gel

Which pairs are correctly matched?

A. (i), (ii), (iv), (v)
B. (i), (iii), (iv)
C. (ii), (iii), (v)
D. (i), (ii), (iii), (iv)
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — Only (iii) is wrong: ice cream is a solid foam (gas in solid), not a sol. A sol is a solid dispersed in a liquid, such as starches and proteins in water.

---

### Q4. A salad dressing manufacturer finds that the oil droplets slowly float to the top of the bottle. Which ingredient addition addresses this, and how?

A. A stabilizer, which thickens the continuous phase so droplets cannot rise
B. An emulsifier, which thickens the continuous phase so droplets cannot rise
C. A stabilizer, which sits at the oil–water interface and coats each droplet
D. Lecithin, which converts the dressing from oil-in-water to water-in-oil
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — Droplets floating to the top is *creaming*. An emulsifier (such as the phospholipid lecithin) coats droplets at the interface; a stabilizer thickens the continuous phase, which is what stops migration in salad dressings.

---

### Q5. Lactose-free 1% milk (made by adding lactase) tastes sweeter than regular 1% milk. Why?

A. Extra sucrose is added during processing to mask off-flavours
B. Lactase splits lactose (index 10–20) into glucose (70–80) and galactose (60), both sweeter than lactose
C. Removing lactose concentrates the remaining milk fat, which tastes sweet
D. Lactase converts lactose into invert sugar, which has an index of 140
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — Splitting a barely-sweet disaccharide into its two sweeter monosaccharides raises perceived sweetness with no change in calories or carbohydrate. Invert sugar (D) is glucose + fructose from *sucrose*, and its index is 100–130.

---

### Q6. Which one of the following is NOT a product of the Maillard reaction?

A. Sugar cane
B. Baked bread
C. Roasted coffee
D. Toast
E. None of the above — all are Maillard products

**Answer: A** — The Maillard reaction requires a reducing sugar *plus* an amino compound (protein); sugar cane is just sugar and has not been browned at all. Bread, coffee and toast all brown by Maillard.

---

### Q7. Granulated sugar is heated alone in a dry pan until it turns golden brown and forms toffee. Which reaction has occurred, and what does it require?

A. Maillard reaction; requires a reducing sugar plus an amino compound
B. Caramelization; requires sugar only
C. Caramelization; requires a reducing sugar plus an amino compound
D. Maillard reaction; requires sugar only
E. Gelatinization; requires starch and water

**Answer: B** — Caramelization is browning of sugar ONLY (caramel, toffee). Maillard needs both sugar and protein, which is why it is the reaction of bread crust, toast, coffee and seared meat.

---

### Q8. When cooked rice, or cooked pasta, turns hard again, the phenomenon is known as:

A. Gelatinization
B. Retrogradation
C. Syneresis
D. Dextrinization
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — Retrogradation is the starch recrystallizing and hardening again. Gelatinization is what cooking does (granules absorb water and swell), and syneresis describes water being squeezed out of a gel.

---

### Q9. Consider the following term/description pairs:

(i) Gelatinization — starch granules absorb water and swell during cooking
(ii) Retrogradation — liquid weeping out of an aged gel
(iii) Syneresis — cooked starch hardens again on storage
(iv) Dextrins — smaller starch fragments
(v) Retrogradation — the main reason bread goes stale

Which pairs are correctly matched?

A. (i), (iv), (v)
B. (i), (ii), (iii)
C. (ii), (iii), (iv)
D. (iii), (iv), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — (ii) and (iii) have their definitions swapped: retrogradation is hardening/recrystallization and syneresis is water being squeezed out of a gel. Stale bread is mostly retrogradation, which is why re-heating with a little water partly re-gelatinizes it.

---

### Q10. Arrange the following sweeteners according to their sweetness index, from highest to lowest: corn syrup, fructose, glucose, lactose, sucrose.

A. Corn syrup > fructose > sucrose > glucose > lactose
B. Corn syrup > fructose > glucose > sucrose > lactose
C. Fructose > corn syrup > sucrose > glucose > lactose
D. Fructose > sucrose > glucose > corn syrup > lactose
E. None of the above — there is not enough information to rank these

**Answer: E** — This is the deck's deliberately tricky question: corn syrup is a variable mixture of glucose and maltose, so it cannot be placed in a ranking. Read sweetness questions carefully before ranking.

---

### Q11. Consider the following hydrocolloids:

(i) agar
(ii) pectin
(iii) alginates
(iv) carrageenan
(v) xanthan gum

Which are extracted from seaweed?

A. (i), (iii), (iv)
B. (i), (ii), (iii)
C. (iii), (iv), (v)
D. (i), (iv)
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — Agar comes from seaweed (kelp), alginates from certain seaweeds and carrageenan from red algae. Pectin is a plant structural polymer (apple) and xanthan gum is produced by bacteria, first isolated from rotting cabbage.

---

### Q12. French fries are quickly prepared by deep fat frying due to oil's ability to:

A. Aerate
B. Carry aroma
C. Emulsify
D. Tenderize
E. Withstand high temperature

**Answer: E** — Of fat's seven functional roles (aerate, carry aroma, emulsify, lubricate, soften, tenderize, withstand high temperature), the one that makes deep frying fast — and gives the crisp crust — is that oil reaches temperatures far above boiling water.

---

### Q13. Which one of the following ingredients in a cup of latte is responsible for the foam?

A. Carbohydrate
B. Lactose
C. Milk fat
D. Milk protein
E. All of the above

**Answer: D** — Protein is the foamer; milk protein stabilizes the gas bubbles. Milk protein is also the natural emulsifier that coats fat droplets and stops commercial milk from creaming.

---

### Q14. Apple sauce has 95% moisture and apple jelly has 35% moisture. Which statement is correct?

A. Apple sauce necessarily has the higher water activity because it has more moisture
B. Moisture content and water activity are two names for the same measurement
C. The jelly's sugar binds water, so moisture content alone is not enough information to rank water activity
D. Apple jelly must have a water activity of 0.35 because water activity equals moisture content divided by 100
E. Both bound and free water are available for microbial growth

**Answer: C** — Water activity measures only the *free* water (scale 0 to 1, pure water = 1.0). Sugar and salt convert free water to bound water, which is why sugary jam and salted fish keep even at moderate moisture; growth needs roughly aw 0.91 for most bacteria, 0.88 for yeasts and 0.80 for molds.

### Q15. Consider the following statements about minor constituents of foods:

(i) Malic acid is the major acid in apple
(ii) A food with a pH below 4.6 is highly acidic (high-acid)
(iii) Ascorbic acid (vitamin C) is used as a bleaching agent in flour whitening
(iv) Calcium sulphate is the coagulant that sets tofu
(v) Citric acid is the major acid in grapes

Which statements are correct?

A. (i), (ii), (iii), (iv)
B. (i), (iii), (v)
C. (ii), (iv), (v)
D. (ii), (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — Only (v) is wrong: tartaric acid belongs to grapes, citric acid to citrus. Vitamin C doubles as a preservative antioxidant (slowing rancidity and browning) and tocopherols (vitamin E) are also antioxidant preservatives.
