# Lesson 09: Biotechnology and Fermentation — Study Deck

35 flashcards · 12 cloze · 15 MCQs

## Flashcards

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define the term "biotechnology". | The use of living organisms, or parts of them, to make products. It covers both ancient fermentation (cheese, beer, soy sauce) and modern genetic engineering. |
| 2 | Which one of the following was NOT a biotechnological (aka genetically modified) product: canola, cheese, insulin, or papaya? | None of them - all four are biotechnology products. This is the classic "all of the above in disguise" answer. |
| 3 | Give examples of biotechnology products named in Lesson 09. | Beer, bovine rennin, flavouring ingredients, miso, soy sauce and xanthan gum - all of them. Also chymosin, vitamins, invertase, glutamic acid, bread, cheddar, yogurt and probiotic drinks (Yakult). |
| 4 | Table 9.1: lactic acid bacteria acting on cucumbers, olives and cabbage give which fermented products? | Cucumbers give dill pickles and sour pickles; olives give green olives and ripe olives; cabbage gives sauerkraut and kimchi. |
| 5 | Table 9.1: lactic acid bacteria acting on milk, dairy and meat give which fermented products? | Milk gives kishk; dairy gives sour cream and yogurt; meat gives meat sausages (salami). |
| 6 | Which two "surprising" commodities in Table 9.1 are fermented by lactic acid bacteria? | Coffee cherries (to coffee beans) and vanilla beans (to vanilla). Both pass through a fermentation step - a favourite exam curveball. |
| 7 | Table 9.1: which microorganisms produce Swiss, Emmenthaler and Gruyere cheeses? | Lactic acid bacteria together with propionic acid bacteria, acting on dairy products. |
| 8 | Table 9.1: lactic acid bacteria with mould acting on vegetable products give what? | Tempeh and soy sauce. |
| 9 | Table 9.1: lactic acid bacteria with mould acting on dairy products give what? | Roquefort, Camembert, Brie and blue cheeses. |
| 10 | Table 9.1: list the yeast fermentations (commodity to product). | Malt to beer, ale and stout; fruit to wine and vermouth; wines to brandy; rice to sake; bread dough to bread. |
| 11 | Which microorganism leads to the production of alcoholic beverages, and how? | Yeast; it converts glucose to ethanol. Not lactic acid bacteria, and not "ethanol to acetic acid" - that is the vinegar step. |
| 12 | How is vinegar produced, and from which Table 9.1 commodity? | Acetic acid bacteria acting on grapes; they convert the ethanol made by yeast into acetic acid. Two different organisms doing two separate steps. |
| 13 | Name the three principles of preservation by fermentation. | Microbial antagonism, antimicrobial agents, and lowering pH. |
| 14 | What is microbial antagonism? | It encourages the growth of alcohol- and acid-producing microorganisms while suppressing spoilage-causing microorganisms. |
| 15 | What is a starter culture? | The deliberate inoculation of the desired microorganisms - commercial yeast, a spoonful of yogurt, a sourdough starter - instead of relying on the right microbes arriving by chance. |
| 16 | Which environmental factors must be controlled to create a favourable environment for fermentation? | Temperature, salt, oxygen and moisture. Kimchi is the example: salt the cabbage, pack it tight to exclude oxygen, and hold it at the right temperature. |
| 17 | Outline the full sequence of microbiological, chemical and physical changes converting milk to cheese. | Pasteurize the milk; add the lactic acid culture (plus other microorganisms if chosen) and the coagulating enzyme (rennet or rennin-like substitute); fermentation converts lactose to lactic acid; curd coagulation as casein micelles form a gel; cutting and cooking; separation into curd and whey. The curd is then matted, milled, salted, pressed, cured and ripened, and packaged for retail. |
| 18 | What is the key chemical change during the fermentation stage of cheese making? | Lactic acid bacteria convert lactose to lactic acid, lowering the pH. |
| 19 | What happens at the curd coagulation stage, and which two physical operations follow it? | Casein micelles that are normally stable in calcium become calcium-sensitive micelles that form a gel. The gel is then cut and cooked, driving out liquid and splitting the mass into curd and whey. |
| 20 | What is contained in the CURD? | Casein, fat, most of the calcium and phosphorus, lactic acid, and water. |
| 21 | What is contained in the WHEY? | Whey proteins, water-soluble vitamins, some calcium and phosphorus, water, lactose, and lactic acid. |
| 22 | After the curd is separated from the whey, what happens to it? | It is matted, milled, salted, pressed, then cured and ripened, and finally packaged for retail. |
| 23 | Why is whey a problem for the dairy industry? | Whey disposal is a major problem faced by the dairy industry because of the high water and solids content of the whey. |
| 24 | Name the enzyme responsible for cheese production and state where it is naturally found. | Rennet, found in the 4th stomach of the calf (the abomasum). Chymosin is the active enzyme within rennet. |
| 25 | What is the major function of rennet? | Formation of a yoghurt-like coagulum - it curdles the milk. It does NOT convert lactose to lactic acid; that is the starter culture's job. |
| 26 | How does industry obtain enough rennet without using calf stomachs? | By genetic engineering: chymosin A is derived from Escherichia coli K-12, and chymosin B from Aspergillus niger or Kluyveromyces marxianus. Permitted cheese enzymes are listed in the Food and Drug Regulations, Division 8, B.08.033. |
| 27 | How does genetically engineered chymosin appear in a cheese ingredient list? | As "microbial enzyme" - e.g. pasteurized milk, salt, bacterial culture, calcium chloride, microbial enzyme. |
| 28 | Give three major reasons for salting cheese. | To draw the whey out of the curd, to flavour the final product, and to inhibit the growth of spoilage-causing microorganisms. |
| 29 | What is NOT a major reason to salt cheese? | To aid the functions of rennet and the starter culture. By the salting step their work is finished, and salt actually slows them down. |
| 30 | Propionibacterium shermanii - which cheese, and what does it do? | Swiss / Emmenthaler. It produces CO2 gas that forms the holes ("eyes") and gives a sweet, nutty flavour. |
| 31 | Penicillium camembertii - which cheese, what does it hydrolyze, and what texture results? | Camembert and Brie. It forms the white, velvet coating on the rind and hydrolyzes PROTEIN to give the creamy texture. |
| 32 | Penicillium roquefortii - which cheese, what does it hydrolyze, and what appearance results? | Roquefort and blue cheeses. It hydrolyzes MILK FAT, giving the blue-veined appearance and a sharp, piquant flavour. |
| 33 | Which two regulatory agencies are responsible for regulating products derived from biotechnology in Canada? | Health Canada and the Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA). Not the WHO, the FAO or the Codex Alimentarius Commission. |
| 34 | What are "novel foods" in Canada? | All three of: (i) products that have never been used as a food, (ii) foods resulting from a process not previously used for food, and (iii) foods modified by genetic manipulation. |
| 35 | What is the Canadian government's overall position on biotechnology-derived foods? | Regulate by product, not by process - assess the safety of the food that actually results rather than banning a technique outright. |

## Cloze facts

- The three principles of preservation by fermentation are {{c1::microbial antagonism}}, {{c2::antimicrobial agents}} and {{c3::lowering pH}}.
- A favourable environment for fermentation is created by controlling {{c1::temperature}}, {{c2::salt}}, {{c3::oxygen}} and {{c4::moisture}}.
- Table 9.1 yeast fermentations: malt gives {{c1::beer, ale and stout}}; fruit gives {{c2::wine and vermouth}}; wines give {{c3::brandy}}; rice gives {{c4::sake}}; bread dough gives {{c5::bread}}.
- In alcoholic fermentation, {{c1::yeast}} converts {{c2::glucose}} to {{c3::ethanol}}; to make vinegar, {{c4::acetic acid bacteria}} then convert ethanol to {{c5::acetic acid}}.
- Cheese making sequence: pasteurization, add {{c1::lactic acid culture}} and other microorganisms if chosen, add the {{c2::coagulating enzyme (rennet or rennin-like substitute)}}, fermentation ({{c3::lactose}} to {{c4::lactic acid}}), curd coagulation (calcium-sensitive casein micelles form a {{c5::gel}}), then {{c6::cutting}} and {{c7::cooking}}, giving curd and whey.

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 09: Biotechnology and Fermentation — Practice MCQs

### Q1. Which one of the following was NOT a biotechnological (aka genetically modified) product?
A. Canola
B. Cheese
C. Insulin
D. Papaya
E. None — all of the above are biotechnology products

**Answer: E** — GM canola, GM papaya, insulin from engineered bacteria and cheese (the ancient one) are all biotechnology products, so there is no exception.

---

### Q2. Which microorganism leads to the production of alcoholic beverages? How?
A. Lactic acid bacteria; it converts lactic acid to ethanol
B. Lactic acid bacteria; it converts glucose to lactic acid
C. Yeast; it converts lactic acid to ethanol
D. Yeast; it converts glucose to ethanol
E. Yeast; it converts ethanol to acetic acid

**Answer: D** — Yeast ferments glucose to ethanol. Option E is the trap: converting ethanol to acetic acid is done by acetic acid bacteria to make vinegar, not by yeast.

---

### Q3. What is microbial antagonism?
A. Encourages the growth of alcohol- and acid-producing microorganisms, as well as spoilage-causing microorganisms
B. Encourages the growth of alcohol- and acid-producing microorganisms, while suppressing spoilage-causing microorganisms
C. Suppresses the growth of alcohol- and acid-producing microorganisms, while encouraging spoilage-causing microorganisms
D. Suppresses the growth of both alcohol- and acid-producing microorganisms and spoilage-causing microorganisms
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — Fermentation deliberately stacks the deck so the desired acid- and alcohol-producers outcompete and suppress the spoilage organisms.

---

### Q4. Which of the following are examples of biotechnology products? (i) Beer (ii) Bovine rennin (iii) Flavouring ingredients (iv) Miso (v) Xanthan gum
A. (ii) only
B. (iii) only
C. (i), (ii)
D. (ii), (v)
E. All of them

**Answer: E** — Every item listed is manufactured with the help of living organisms or their parts, so all of them qualify as biotechnology products.

---

### Q5. Which two regulatory agencies are responsible for the regulation of products derived from biotechnology in Canada? (i) Health Canada (ii) Canadian Food Inspection Agency (iii) World Health Organization (iv) Food and Agriculture Organization (v) Codex Alimentarius Commission
A. (i), (ii)
B. (i), (iii)
C. (i), (iv)
D. (i), (v)
E. (ii), (v)

**Answer: A** — Health Canada and the CFIA are the domestic pair. WHO, FAO and Codex are international bodies and do not regulate Canadian products.

---

### Q6. What are novel foods in Canada? (i) Products that have never been used as a food (ii) Foods resulting from a process that has not previously been used for food (iii) Foods that have been modified by genetic manipulation
A. (i)
B. (iii)
C. (i), (iii)
D. (ii), (iii)
E. (i), (ii), (iii)

**Answer: E** — All three routes make a food "novel." Genetic modification is only one of the three; a brand-new process qualifies a food even with zero genetic engineering.

---

### Q7. (i) Name the enzyme responsible for cheese production. (ii) Where is it naturally found?
A. (i) Rennet — (ii) along with casein
B. (i) Rennet — (ii) 4th stomach in calf
C. (i) Chymosin — (ii) along with casein
D. (i) Chymosin — (ii) 4th stomach in calf
E. (i) Lipase — (ii) 4th stomach in calf

**Answer: B** — The pairing the exam wants is rennet plus the 4th stomach of the calf. Chymosin is the active enzyme *within* rennet, so option D is the near-miss distractor.

---

### Q8. What is the major function of rennet?
A. Converts lactose to lactic acid and leads to a mildly acidic environment
B. Creates a favourable environment for all starter culture
C. Aids in the digestion of grass in the calf's 4th stomach
D. Aids in the formation of a yoghurt-like coagulum
E. All of the above

**Answer: D** — Rennet coagulates the milk. Option A is what the starter culture (lactic acid bacteria) does — acidifying and coagulating are two different jobs by two different agents.

---

### Q9. What is NOT a major reason to salt cheese?
A. To draw the whey out of the curd
B. To flavour the final product
C. To inhibit growth of spoilage-causing microorganisms
D. To aid the functions of rennet and the starter culture
E. None of the above — all four are major reasons

**Answer: D** — Salting does draw out whey, flavour the cheese and inhibit spoilage organisms, but by the salting step the rennet and starter culture have finished their work and salt slows them down.

---

### Q10. \_\_\_(i)\_\_\_ is responsible for the white, velvet coating on Camembert cheese. It hydrolyzes \_\_\_(ii)\_\_\_ to form the \_\_\_(iii)\_\_\_ texture.
A. (i) Propionibacterium shermanii, (ii) protein, (iii) hole and sweet
B. (i) Penicillium roquefortii, (ii) milk fat, (iii) blue-veined appeal
C. (i) Penicillium camembertii, (ii) protein, (iii) creamy
D. (i) Penicillium camembertii, (ii) milk fat, (iii) creamy
E. (i) Penicillium roquefortii, (ii) protein, (iii) creamy

**Answer: C** — *P. camembertii* makes the white rind on Camembert and Brie and hydrolyzes protein for the creamy texture. *P. roquefortii* is the blue-cheese organism and hydrolyzes milk fat.

---

### Q11. Which of the following end up mainly in the WHEY rather than the curd? (i) Casein (ii) Whey proteins (iii) Lactose (iv) Most of the calcium and phosphorus (v) Water-soluble vitamins
A. (i), (iv)
B. (ii), (iii), (v)
C. (ii), (iv), (v)
D. (i), (ii), (iii)
E. All of them

**Answer: B** — Whey holds whey proteins, water-soluble vitamins, lactose, lactic acid, water and only *some* calcium and phosphorus. Casein, fat and *most* of the calcium and phosphorus stay in the curd.

---

### Q12. According to Table 9.1, which of the following commodities are fermented by lactic acid bacteria? (i) Coffee cherries (ii) Grapes (iii) Vanilla beans (iv) Cabbage (v) Malt
A. (i), (iii), (iv)
B. (ii), (v)
C. (i), (ii), (iii)
D. (iii), (iv), (v)
E. All of them

**Answer: A** — Coffee cherries, vanilla beans and cabbage are lactic acid bacteria fermentations. Grapes to vinegar is acetic acid bacteria, and malt to beer is yeast.

---

### Q13. A Swiss cheese ingredient list reads "pasteurized milk, salt, bacterial culture, calcium chloride, microbial enzyme." What is the "microbial enzyme" most likely to be?
A. Lactic acid bacteria added as the starter culture
B. Chymosin produced by genetically engineered microorganisms such as *E. coli* K-12 or *Aspergillus niger*
C. Lipase from *P. roquefortii* used to hydrolyze milk fat
D. Propionic acid bacteria that generate the eyes in the cheese
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — Calf stomachs cannot supply world demand, so chymosin A is made by *E. coli* K-12 and chymosin B by *Aspergillus niger* or *Kluyveromyces marxianus*; on labels this is declared as "microbial enzyme."

---

### Q14. Which sequence correctly describes the conversion of milk to cheese?
A. Pasteurize → add rennet → add lactic acid culture → cure and ripen → cut and cook → separate curd and whey
B. Pasteurize → add lactic acid culture and coagulating enzyme → fermentation (lactose to lactic acid) → curd coagulation → cut and cook → separate curd and whey → mat, mill, salt, press, cure and ripen
C. Add lactic acid culture → pasteurize → cut and cook → curd coagulation → salt → press → separate curd and whey
D. Pasteurize → add lactic acid culture → salt → press → add rennet → curd coagulation → cure and ripen
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — Pasteurization comes first, both the culture and the coagulating enzyme go in before fermentation, and salting/pressing/curing happen only after the curd has been separated from the whey.

---

### Q15. Why is whey a significant issue for the dairy industry?
A. Whey disposal is a major problem because of the high water and solids content of the whey
B. Whey contains all of the casein and fat, so discarding it wastes the protein
C. Whey must be pasteurized a second time before the cheese can be sold
D. Whey lowers the pH of the curd below 4.6 and spoils the cheese
E. All of the above

**Answer: A** — The lecture flags whey disposal as a major problem faced by the dairy industry because of the high water and solids content. Casein and fat stay in the curd, not the whey, which is why B is wrong.
