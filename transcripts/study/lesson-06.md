# Lesson 06: Thermal Processing — Study Deck

## Flashcards

| # | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What thermal process is used when apple slices are dipped in hot water to prevent enzymatic browning, and what does it target? | Blanching, a mild heat treatment that inactivates enzymes such as polyphenol oxidase. It is also what is done to vegetables before freezing. |
| 2 | What is the main purpose of pasteurizing milk? | To eliminate pathogenic (ugly) microorganisms. It does not target spoilage organisms, psychrotrophs, or enzymes, which is why pasteurized milk still spoils and needs refrigeration. |
| 3 | What does commercial sterilization aim for, and which organism is it aimed at? | It aims for 'complete' elimination of all microorganisms, targeting Clostridium botulinum spores. It is achieved by canning and UHT (ultra-high temperature). |
| 4 | Which of these does commercial sterilization target: spoilage organisms, pathogens, spores, thermophilic spores? Why are thermophilic spores excluded? | It targets spoilage-causing microorganisms, pathogenic microorganisms and spores, but NOT thermophilic spores. Thermophiles need about 50 C or more to grow, so survivors sit harmlessly at storage temperature - hence commercial rather than absolute sterility. |
| 5 | Canning vs UHT: what is the difference in the order of operations? | Canning: food is placed in the container and sealed FIRST, then the whole package is heated. UHT: the food is heated up first at ultra-high temperature, THEN filled into the container. |
| 6 | Do the containers have to be sterilized before filling in canning vs in UHT? | Canning containers only need to be clean, because the sealed package is heated afterwards. UHT containers must be fully sterilized beforehand. |
| 7 | What is aseptic packaging? | The food and the package are sterilized separately and then combined (filled and sealed) in a sterile environment. It is what makes UHT products shelf-stable. |
| 8 | What is the official name of the juice-box packaging material, and how is it most commonly sterilized? | Tetra Pak, sterilized with hydrogen peroxide. Not ascorbic acid, carbon dioxide, pressurized steam, or superheated steam. |
| 9 | Give examples of canned products vs UHT-aseptically packaged products. | Canning: SPAM, canned tuna, soup, canned peaches. UHT-aseptic: shelf-stable Parmalat milk (longest shelf life of the milks shown), ketchup, sriracha, protein shakes, latte pouches. |
| 10 | Packaging pros and cons: tin can and glass jar. | Tin can: durable, cheap, opaque, but heavy and can corrode. Glass jar: inert, resealable, see-through, but heavy and breakable. |
| 11 | Packaging pros and cons: Tetra Pak and retortable pouch. | Tetra Pak: light and cheap to ship, but not resealable and hard to recycle. Retortable pouch: light and heats fast, but can puncture. |
| 12 | Choosing between blanching, pasteurization and sterilization depends on what three things? | The type of food (chemical, taste, usage), the storage conditions, and the targeted microorganisms. |
| 13 | How do you recognize a logarithmic graph, and why is one used for survivor counts? | Its gridlines bunch up and are labelled in powers of ten (1, 10, 100, 1000, 10000) instead of even intervals (0, 10, 20, 30...). Each heating interval kills the same percentage, not the same number (6000 to 600 to 60 to 6), so a log axis turns survivors vs time into a straight line. |
| 14 | Distinguish the thermal death RATE curve from the thermal death TIME curve. | Thermal death rate curve: number of survivors (log scale) vs time at one temperature; its slope gives the D-value. Thermal death time curve: D-value (log scale) vs temperature; it gives the Z-value. |
| 15 | Define the D-value, and state when it stays constant. | D-value (decimal reduction time) is the time it takes to kill 90% of the microorganisms, i.e. one log cycle. It is fixed for the same microorganism, in the same food, at the same temperature - but it changes at different processing temperatures. |
| 16 | On thermal death rate curves, how does the slope relate to the D-value, and which organisms are most resistant? | Steeper line = faster death = smaller D-value, so the shallowest line (e.g. C among A, B, C) has the largest D-value. Ranked by resistance: vegetative cells (steepest) < C. botulinum spores < spores of thermophilic bacteria (shallowest). |
| 17 | Starting with 6000 microbes and D = 5 min, list the survivors at 0, 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 minutes. | 6000, 600, 60, 6, 0.6, 0.06. One log cycle is removed every 5 minutes. |
| 18 | Chicken broth (pH 6.2) had 4,000 units of bacteria; after 18 min at 98 C only 4 units were left. What is the D-value? | 4000 -> 400 -> 40 -> 4 = 3 log cycles. D = 18 min / 3 = 6 minutes. |
| 19 | Chicken broth, pH 6.2, D = 6 min. According to common practice, how long should it be processed? | pH 6.2 is at or above 4.6, so it is low-acid -> 12D. 12 x 6 min = 72 minutes. |
| 20 | A pathogen has a decimal reduction time of 5 minutes in tomato juice at 91 C. How long should the juice (pH 3.8) be processed to sterilize it? | pH 3.8 is below 4.6, so it is high-acid -> 5D. 5 x 5 min = 25 minutes. |
| 21 | Bacterial load fell from 40,000 to 4,000 units in 4 minutes. Find D, and the total time for (i) 4 units, (ii) 0.4 units, (iii) 5D, (iv) 12D. | 40,000 -> 4,000 is 1 log cycle in 4 min, so D = 4 min. (i) 4 logs = 16 min; (ii) 5 logs = 20 min (iii) 5D = 20 min; (iv) 12D = 48 min. |
| 22 | State the pH 4.6 rule for processing time. | pH equal to or above 4.6 (low-acid): longer processing time, 12D (12 times the D-value). pH below 4.6 (high-acid): shorter processing time, 5D (5 times the D-value). |
| 23 | What is the '12D botulinum cook' and why is it considered enough? | Twelve log reductions of C. botulinum in low-acid food. Starting from one spore per can, the odds of a survivor are about one in a trillion, giving safety without ever having to prove sterility. |
| 24 | Define the Z-value, and say what a small Z-value implies. | Z-value is the change in temperature that causes a 10x change (one log cycle) in the D-value. A small Z-value means the organism is very responsive to temperature. |
| 25 | A pathogen has D = 5 min (300 s) at 91 C and Z = 5 C. What is its D-value at 121 C? | 121 - 91 = 30 C; 30 / 5 = 6 log cycles. 300 s / 10^6 = 0.0003 seconds. |
| 26 | Tomato juice: D = 7 min (420 s) at 91 C, Z = 5 C. Give D at 96, 101, 106, 111, 116 and 121 C. | Every 5 C rise divides D by 10. 42, 4.2, 0.42, 0.042, 0.0042, 0.00042 seconds. |
| 27 | Tomato juice at pH 4.4 has D = 0.00042 s at 121 C. What is the F-value? | pH 4.4 is below 4.6, so high-acid -> 5D. F = 5 x 0.00042 = 0.0021 seconds. |
| 28 | Pineapple juice heated at 96 C for 30 min: Bacteria A went 48,000 -> 4,800 (Z = 10 C), Bacteria B went 150,000 -> 150 (Z = 5 C). Find their D-values at 96, 106 and 86 C. | A: 1 log in 30 min -> D = 30 min; at 106 C = 3 min; at 86 C = 300 min. B: 3 logs in 30 min -> D = 10 min; at 106 C (10/5 = 2 cycles) = 0.1 min; at 86 C = 1000 min. |
| 29 | Define the F-value. | A mathematically calculated number describing the total lethal effect of the process at the slowest heating point in the container. It is the time required to sterilize a selected organism at the reference temperature 121.1 C (250 F). |
| 30 | What are the three steps for calculating an F-value? | 1) Convert the D-value to 121 C using the Z-value. 2) Check the pH of the food to decide 5D or 12D. 3) Multiply. Convert first, then decide 5D or 12D. |
| 31 | Ranch dressing, pH 5.3, D = 3 min (180 s) at 91 C, Z = 10 C. What is the F-value? | 30 C / 10 = 3 log cycles, so 180 s -> 0.18 s at 121 C. pH 5.3 is above 4.6 -> 12D; F = 12 x 0.18 = 2.16 seconds. |
| 32 | What assumptions and precautions create the margin of safety in thermal processing? | Assume the most commonly found toxic microorganism C. botulinum is present, assume it is in its most resistant form (spores), and design the process on an even more heat-resistant relative. Plus: C. botulinum does not grow in acid, and sanitation minimizes the starting contamination. |
| 33 | Conduction heating: how does heat move, in which foods, and where is the cold point? | Heat transfers molecule to molecule in straight lines, in solid foods such as SPAM and pumpkin puree. The cold point is at the geometric centre of the container. |
| 34 | Convection heating: how does heat move, in which foods, and where is the cold point? | Heat transfers by fluid motion - the fluid heats along the hot container walls and rises - in thin liquids such as broth and juice. It is much faster and the cold point sits lower, below the centre. |
| 35 | How is heat transferred in a can of corn chowder, and what about cranberry sauce that gels during processing? | Corn chowder: mainly convection, with some conduction. Cranberry sauce starts as a liquid heated by convection and gels partway through, switching to conduction. |

## Cloze facts

- The three heat treatments and their targets: {{c1::blanching}} targets {{c1::enzymes such as polyphenol oxidase}}, {{c2::pasteurization}} targets {{c2::pathogenic (ugly) microorganisms}}, and {{c3::commercial sterilization}} targets essentially everything, especially {{c3::Clostridium botulinum spores}}.
- In {{c1::canning}} the food is placed and sealed in the container first and then heated, while in {{c2::UHT}} the food is heated first and then filled into the container; canning containers only need to be {{c3::clean}} whereas UHT containers must be {{c3::fully sterilized}} beforehand.
- The official name of the juice-box packaging material is {{c1::Tetra Pak}}, and it is most commonly sterilized with {{c2::hydrogen peroxide}}; combining separately sterilized food and package is called {{c3::aseptic packaging}}.
- The {{c1::D-value}}, or {{c1::decimal reduction time}}, is the time needed to kill {{c2::90}}% of the microorganisms, i.e. {{c2::one}} log cycle; it is fixed for the same {{c3::microorganism}}, in the same {{c3::food}}, at the same {{c3::temperature}}.
- Food at pH equal to or above {{c1::4.6}} is {{c1::low-acid}} and is processed for {{c2::12D}}, while food at pH below 4.6 is {{c1::high-acid}} and is processed for {{c3::5D}}.
- On a thermal death {{c1::rate}} curve, {{c2::number of survivors}} is plotted against {{c2::time}}, while on a thermal death {{c1::time}} curve the {{c3::D-value}} is plotted against {{c3::temperature}}.
- The {{c1::Z-value}} is the change in temperature that causes a {{c2::10x}} change (one log cycle) in the {{c2::D-value}}; a small Z-value means the organism is {{c3::very responsive to temperature}}.
- The {{c1::F-value}} is the total processing time needed to sterilize a specific organism at the reference temperature {{c2::121.1}} C ({{c2::250}} F), measured at the {{c3::slowest heating point}} in the container; to calculate it, convert the D-value to 121 C using the {{c1::Z-value}}, check the {{c2::pH}} to decide {{c3::5D or 12D}}, then multiply.
- Chicken broth at pH {{c1::6.2}} went from 4,000 to 4 units in 18 min, which is {{c2::3}} log cycles, so D = {{c2::6}} min; being low-acid it needs 12D = {{c3::72}} minutes, whereas tomato juice at pH 3.8 with D = 5 min needs 5D = {{c3::25}} minutes.
- Ranch dressing, pH 5.3, D = 180 s at 91 C with Z = 10 C: 30 C / 10 = {{c1::3}} log cycles, so D at 121 C = {{c1::0.18}} s; pH 5.3 is above 4.6 so use {{c2::12D}}, giving F = {{c3::2.16}} seconds.
- The margin of safety assumes {{c1::C. botulinum}} is present, assumes it is in its most resistant form, {{c2::spores}}, and designs the process on an even more {{c3::heat-resistant 'relative'}} of C. botulinum.
- Heat moves molecule to molecule in straight lines by {{c1::conduction}} in solid foods like SPAM, where the cold point is at the {{c2::geometric centre}}; it moves by fluid motion via {{c1::convection}} in thin liquids, where the cold point is {{c2::lower than the centre}}; a can of corn chowder is {{c3::mainly convection with some conduction}}.

## Practice MCQs (instructor style)

# FNH 200 — Lesson 06: Thermal Processing
## Practice MCQs

### Q1. In Lesson 05 we learned that we can dip apple slices in hot water to prevent enzymatic browning. Name this thermal process.
A. Blanching
B. Pasteurization
C. Commercial sterilization
D. Aseptic packaging
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — Blanching is the mild heat treatment that inactivates enzymes such as polyphenol oxidase; it is also applied to vegetables before freezing.

### Q2. What is the main purpose to pasteurize milk?
A. To inactivate polyphenol oxidase
B. To eliminate pathogenic microorganisms
C. To eliminate spoilage-causing microorganisms
D. To eliminate psychrotrophic spoilage-causing microorganisms
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — Pasteurization is a moderate heat treatment aimed at pathogenic ("ugly") microorganisms only. Spoilage organisms and psychrotrophs survive, which is why pasteurized milk is refrigerated and still spoils.

### Q3. What are the targeted microorganisms in commercial sterilization?
(i) Spoilage-causing microorganisms
(ii) Pathogenic microorganisms
(iii) Spores
(iv) Thermophilic spores
(v) Enzymes such as polyphenol oxidase

A. (i), (ii)
B. (i), (ii), (iii)
C. (i), (ii), (iii), (iv)
D. (ii), (iii), (iv)
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — Commercial sterilization targets spoilage organisms, pathogens and spores, especially *Clostridium botulinum* spores. Thermophilic spores are deliberately left out because thermophiles need roughly 50 °C or more to grow, so survivors are harmless in the pantry; enzymes are the target of blanching.

### Q4. Both canning and UHT lead to the complete sterilization of the food product. What is the major difference?
A. In UHT, food is first placed and sealed in a container before heat is applied.
B. In UHT, food is heated up first before placing in a container.
C. In UHT, the container is heated up first before food is placed inside.
D. In UHT, ultra high temperature is applied to the whole package.
E. None of the above

**Answer: B** — Canning is "food in first, then heat"; UHT is "heated first, then filled." Because the UHT container never passes through a retort with the food, it must be fully sterilized beforehand — the basis of aseptic packaging.

### Q5. What is the most common way to sterilize a Tetra Pak?
A. Ascorbic acid
B. Carbon dioxide
C. Hydrogen peroxide
D. Pressurized steam
E. None of the above

**Answer: C** — Tetra Pak is the official name of the juice-box material, and hydrogen peroxide is the standard sterilant; steam options are the classic distractors here.

### Q6. A sample of chicken broth (pH 6.2) had 4,000 units of bacteria; after 18 minutes of treatment at 98 °C, 4 units were left. What is the D-value?
A. 3 minutes
B. 4.5 minutes
C. 6 minutes
D. 9 minutes
E. 18 minutes

**Answer: C** — 4,000 → 400 → 40 → 4 is three log cycles, so D = 18 min ÷ 3 = 6 minutes. Choosing 4.5 comes from dividing by four "steps" instead of three log reductions.

### Q7. Using the chicken broth above (pH 6.2, D = 6 min), how long should it be processed according to common practice?
A. 6 minutes
B. 18 minutes
C. 24 minutes
D. 30 minutes
E. 72 minutes

**Answer: E** — pH 6.2 is at or above 4.6, so the food is low-acid and needs the 12D botulinum cook: 12 × 6 = 72 minutes. Option D (30 min) is the trap of applying 5D to a low-acid food.

### Q8. A pathogenic microorganism has a decimal reduction time of 5 minutes in tomato juice when processed at 91 °C. How long should it be processed to sterilize the juice (pH = 3.8)?
A. 25 seconds
B. 60 seconds
C. 5 minutes
D. 25 minutes
E. 60 minutes

**Answer: D** — pH 3.8 is below 4.6, so the juice is high-acid and 5D is sufficient: 5 × 5 min = 25 minutes. Option E (60 min) is 12D applied to the wrong pH class.

### Q9. A pathogenic microorganism has a decimal reduction time of 5 minutes in tomato juice at 91 °C. If this juice has a Z-value of 5 °C, what is its D-value at 121 °C?
A. 0.0003 seconds
B. 0.003 seconds
C. 0.03 seconds
D. 0.3 seconds
E. 5 seconds

**Answer: A** — 121 − 91 = 30 °C, and 30 ÷ 5 = 6 log cycles, so D falls by 10⁶: 300 s ÷ 1,000,000 = 0.0003 seconds. Working in minutes instead of seconds is what produces the other options.

### Q10. A pathogenic microorganism has a D-value of 3 minutes in a ranch dressing (pH 5.3) when processed at 91 °C and a Z-value of 10 °C. What is its F-value?
A. 1.8 seconds
B. 0.18 seconds
C. 0.90 seconds
D. 2.16 seconds
E. 21.6 seconds

**Answer: D** — 30 °C ÷ 10 = 3 log cycles, so 180 s becomes 0.18 s at 121 °C; pH 5.3 is above 4.6, so multiply by 12D: 12 × 0.18 = 2.16 s. Option B is the D-value at 121 °C (forgetting the 5D/12D step) and option C is 5D wrongly applied.

### Q11. What precautionary steps are in place to ensure the margin of safety is as large as possible?
(i) *Clostridium botulinum* does not grow in an acidic environment
(ii) Necessary sanitary steps are in place to minimize contamination
(iii) The time it takes to destroy *C. botulinum* spores is determined based on a highly heat-resistant spore
(iv) The process is designed assuming *C. botulinum* is absent from the raw ingredients
(v) *C. botulinum* is assumed to be present in its most resistant form, spores

A. (i), (ii)
B. (i), (iii), (v)
C. (i), (ii), (iii)
D. (i), (ii), (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: D** — The margin of safety is stacked by assuming *C. botulinum* is present, assuming it is in spore form, and designing the process on an even more heat-resistant relative, alongside acidity and sanitation. Statement (iv) is the opposite of what is assumed, so "All of the above" is wrong.

### Q12. Which one of the following is a thermal death rate curve?
A. Number of survivors plotted against time
B. D-value plotted against temperature
C. D-value plotted against time
D. Number of survivors plotted against temperature
E. None of the above

**Answer: A** — The thermal death *rate* curve plots survivors (log scale) against time at a fixed temperature and its slope gives the D-value. Option B describes the thermal death *time* curve, which gives the Z-value.

### Q13. Consider three thermal death rate curves A, B and C, where C is the shallowest (least steep) line. Which microorganism has the largest D-value?
A. A
B. B
C. C
D. All three have the same D-value because they start from the same population
E. Cannot be determined without knowing the processing temperature

**Answer: C** — A steeper line means faster death and a smaller D-value, so the shallowest line has the largest D-value and is the most heat-resistant organism. This is the same logic that puts thermophilic spores above *C. botulinum* spores above vegetative cells on the lecture graph.

### Q14. Describe how heat is transferred in a can of corn chowder.
A. Conduction
B. Convection
C. Mainly conduction; some convection
D. Mainly convection; some conduction
E. None of the above

**Answer: D** — Corn chowder is a thin liquid carrying solid chunks, so fluid currents dominate with some molecule-to-molecule transfer through the solids. Cranberry sauce is the reverse trap: it starts as convection and switches to conduction once it gels during processing.

### Q15. Which of the following are heated mainly by conduction, with the cold point at the geometric centre of the container?
(i) A can of SPAM
(ii) Pumpkin purée
(iii) Chicken broth
(iv) Apple juice
(v) Cranberry sauce after it has gelled during processing

A. (i), (iii)
B. (i), (ii), (v)
C. (ii), (iii), (iv)
D. (i), (ii), (iii), (v)
E. All of the above

**Answer: B** — Solid or gelled foods conduct heat molecule to molecule in straight lines and their cold point sits at the geometric centre. Thin liquids such as broth and juice heat by convection, which is faster and puts the cold point lower than the centre.
